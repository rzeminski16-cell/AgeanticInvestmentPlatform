At that scale the capitalisation and depreciation policy, not revenue recognition, is the decisive earnings-quality variable: the split between infrastructure cost capitalised and cost expensed, and the useful lives applied to server and network equipment, set how much of the current build is charged against current operating income, and a longer assumed life flatters margins with no change in cash outlay. ### Recorded Caveats

- More than three quarters of the enterprise value is terminal value. The explicit forecast is contributing little, so the answer rests almost entirely on the terminal assumption rather than on the projected years a reader can check.
- The two terminal methods disagree by more than a quarter. That is information, not an error: they are two different guesses about the same unknowable quantity, and the distance between them is the honest width of the answer.

### Commentary

The equity value this build reaches, $3,296.2bn, follows less from the operating detail beneath it than from a small set of structural choices: how the terminal period is treated, what discount rate is applied to it, and what relationship the forecast sets between capital spend and depreciation. Each deserves to be weighed on its own, because each moves the answer further than the revenue growth path does.

Start with the terminal treatment. The two terminal figures do not bracket a range; they disagree. The exit-multiple terminal value of $5,345.9bn stands above the entire recorded enterprise value of $3,315.5bn, while the Gordon growth terminal value of $1,861.5bn falls well below it. A reader choosing between the terminal value share lines, or between the two value-per-share lines, is choosing the answer rather than refining it. Nor do the two corroborate each other: the implied exit multiple of 5.57x is the multiple the Gordon case effectively pays for the terminal year, and it is not the exit multiple the other treatment applies.

Then the growth assumed in perpetuity. Implied terminal growth of 7.89% sits only modestly below the cost of equity of 9.74%, and not far below the forecast CAGR of 10.94%. That is not a steady state; it is the explicit forecast's growth extended without decay, and it is far above any rate an economy can sustain indefinitely. The consequence is structural. Most of the value sits beyond the explicit window — the terminal value share components above make the proportion plain — and the terminal-year discount factor of 0.601 shows how lightly discounting erodes it. At that narrow spacing between terminal growth and WACC, a modest revision to either moves equity value by more than any defensible revision to EBIT margin, capex intensity or working capital intensity in the explicit years.

Cash conversion is the third pressure point, and the one with most leverage inside the forecast window. Capex intensity and depreciation intensity are carried as separate inputs, and depreciation is held well below capital spend throughout. That single relationship flatters EBIT margin and free cash flow together. Its scale shows against the filed year: forecast free cash flow of $139.2bn exceeds what the filed year generated once capital expenditure of $115.9bn is set against operating cash flow of $182.9bn, and forecast EBITDA of $334.1bn is of the same order as the whole of FY2026 revenue at $331.8bn. On a path where depreciation converges toward the capital spend the filed accounts already show, forecast EBIT margin and free cash flow would both be lower, and every output above with them. We would read the base case as the optimistic end of a range on this input, not its midpoint. The forecast change in working capital of $18.2bn is a second-order item beside it.

The filed operating record gives partial support and partial warning. In FY2026 against the prior year, revenue grew strongly on cloud demand, but cost of revenue grew faster than revenue and gross margin dollars grew more slowly than revenue — the direction of travel in the margin line is downward while the capital build continues, which is the opposite of the widening the terminal case requires. Management states that existing cash, short-term investments, operating cash flow and capital markets access are expected to fund operations and commitments, including material capital expenditures, for at least the next 12 months and thereafter, so the spending path is fundable; fundable is not the same as accretive at these assumed returns.

Finally, what these figures are and are not. They are absolute outputs of one build under stated inputs, and the components above express no verdict on the traded shares; they should be read as the set of conditions that must hold for the value shown, not as a target price, and the price comparison belongs elsewhere in this report. The company applies the same discounted cash flow machinery in its own goodwill testing and acknowledges there that the estimation of future cash flows, of the long-term growth rate and of the weighted average cost of capital each require significant judgement. That is the honest characterisation here too: the conclusion is dominated by the terminal assumptions and the depreciation-to-capex relationship, and a reader who disputes either is disputing the valuation itself.
