What it intends to do

Standard point-in-time run on M&T Bank Corporation (MTB, NYSE), USD, 12-month horizon, using only material published on or before 2026-09-12; the latest completed reporting period is the quarter ended 2026-06-30. Built around three operator questions: CRE exposure and reserve coverage; whether NIM is at a cyclical peak and what deposit repricing does to it; whether ROTCE justifies the price-to-tangible-book multiple. Evidence is primary-regulatory first (FY2023-FY2025 10-Ks, Q1/Q2 2026 10-Qs, 8-K supplements, FR Y-9C, Call Reports, UBPR). Revised after review: peer CRE and capital data now come from peers' own FR Y-9C/UBPR schedule lines rather than non-uniform 10-Q tables; cost of equity is explicitly sourced and recorded as a labelled assumption, with implied P/TBV shown across a range rather than a point; traded P/TBV is paired with reported ROTCE across the full history, not only at spot; and the disclosed rate-shock simulation is treated as a management model output, cross-checked against realised deposit betas computed by code from average-balance tables. No figure is stated in this plan; all numbers are computed by code from stored facts.

Sections it will write

  • Balance Sheet & Liquidity balance_sheet_liquidity Answers question (a). Loan mix by class from the 10-K/10-Q loan tables: CRE (construction, non-owner-occupied, multifamily, owner-occupied) against total loans and against total risk-based capital, plus the supervisory concentration screens computed on FR Y-9C lines. CECL allowance rollforward and allocation by class, nonaccrual, criticized/classified, net charge-offs and delinquency by class. Funding side for question (b): deposit mix (noninterest-bearing, interest-bearing, time), uninsured share, brokered funding, borrowings, securities portfolio, AOCI and HTM unrealized position, liquidity and capital ratios.
  • Earnings Quality earnings_quality Answers question (b) at the reported level. Decompose net interest income into average earning-asset balances, asset yields, interest-bearing liability costs and margin from the average-balance tables. Separate durable spread from items that flatter it: purchase-accounting accretion, day-count and reset effects, securities repositioning gains, reserve build/release, non-recurring fee and expense items. Have code compute realised interest-bearing deposit betas quarter by quarter from those tables against observed policy-rate moves, so the deposit-repricing answer rests on observed behaviour. All forward NIM commentary marked unverified guidance.
  • Historical Financial Analysis historical_financial_analysis Multi-year factual base from FY2023-FY2025 10-Ks and 2026 interims: net interest income and margin, provision and net charge-offs, fee income, expense and efficiency, net income to common, ROA, ROCE, ROTCE, tangible common equity, tangible book value per share, share count. Establish the observed peak-to-trough path of margin and CRE balances. Added after review: pair the quarterly price series with the same-period reported TBV per share and ROTCE so code produces a historical P/TBV and P/TBV-to-ROTCE series, giving the spot multiple a reference against MTB's own cycle.
  • Valuation — Discounted Cash Flow valuation_dcf Answers question (c). A free-cash-flow DCF is not a valid frame for a bank holding company; the intended method is residual income on tangible common equity with a dividend-discount cross-check, output as an implied P/TBV from sustainable ROTCE versus cost of equity. Cost of equity has no filed provenance: it is built from the as-of-date Treasury yield, a documented published ERP and a beta estimated by code over a fixed price window, recorded as a labelled assumption. Implied P/TBV must be shown across a stated range of costs of equity and compared to both the traded multiple at the as-of date and the historical P/TBV series.
  • Scenarios & Sensitivities scenarios_sensitivities Parameterise the two quantitative questions. For NIM: deposit-beta scenarios on the down-rate path, asset repricing and fixed-rate rollover. The 10-K/10-Q simulated net interest income change for parallel shocks is used as a reference point but labelled a management model output whose beta, repricing and balance-growth assumptions are undisclosed and not reproducible; it is presented alongside the independently computed realised deposit betas. For credit: CRE loss-severity scenarios on office, multifamily and construction balances, benchmarked to disclosed criticized balances and supervisory stressed loss rates. Assumptions labelled as assumptions.
  • Industry & Competitive Positioning industry_landscape Peer context for the CRE and multiple questions, built for like-for-like comparison. CRE-to-total-capital, allowance-to-loans and asset-quality metrics for the peer set are drawn from the peers' own FR Y-9C/UBPR schedule lines, the same lines used for MTB; peer 10-Qs are used only for items regulatory reports do not carry (tangible common equity, share count). State the peer-selection rule and the published vintage of every regulatory report used, and state any residual definitional difference (owner-occupied, held-for-sale, construction treatment) that survives the common-schedule approach. Include supervisory concentration thresholds.
  • Segment Analysis segment_analysis Map reportable segments as defined in the FY2025 10-K segment footnote, with segment net interest income, provision, revenue and average loans and deposits. Reconcile where CRE exposure sits by segment and flag any segment redefinition across the review period that breaks comparability. Include geographic footprint and deposit-share context only where a filed source supports it, since regional concentration bears directly on the CRE loss question.
  • Capital Allocation capital_allocation Capital return and its interaction with the book-value multiple: CET1, internal target and the stress capital buffer in force at the as-of date, dividend declarations, repurchase authorisation and actual repurchases by quarter, the effect of buybacks on tangible book value per share and share count, and AOCI accretion. Cover acquisition-related capital use and any transaction announced in 8-Ks through 2026-09-12. Tie payout and retention to the residual-income inputs so the valuation section and this section use one set of stored facts.
  • Investment Thesis investment_thesis State the thesis as falsifiable propositions tied to the three operator questions: whether CRE reserve coverage is adequate relative to observed criticized migration; whether the margin is peaking, plateauing or still expanding under the prevailing rate path; and whether normalised ROTCE clears an assumed cost of equity by enough to justify the traded multiple of tangible book, stated across the cost-of-equity range rather than at one point. Each proposition carries the specific filed evidence that would confirm or break it and the section presenting it. No figures here; references only.
  • Key Risks key_risks Company-level risks over the 12-month horizon: CRE credit migration concentrated in office and construction, deposit-cost stickiness compressing spread if rates fall faster than assets reprice, securities and AOCI marks, regulatory capital change, Northeast/Mid-Atlantic concentration, acquisition and integration risk, and operational or legal matters from the 10-K risk factors and legal proceedings note. Distinguish risks the filings disclose from risks proposed by the analyst and not yet evidenced.
  • Catalysts catalysts Dated, checkable events in the next twelve months: the Q3 2026 earnings release and 10-Q (after the as-of date, therefore outside the evidence set but inside the horizon), subsequent quarterly releases, dividend declaration dates, buyback authorisation renewal, the next supervisory stress-test cycle and stress capital buffer reset, and scheduled policy-rate decisions that drive deposit repricing. Each item flagged as scheduled-and-unknown rather than forecast.
  • Executive Summary executive_summary Written last, from validated section outputs only. One paragraph per operator question, each stating the answer, the confidence, the single piece of filed evidence it rests on, and the condition that would reverse it. Where the answer depends on an assumed input with no filed provenance (cost of equity, rate path, loss severity), say so in the same sentence as the answer. Every figure is a reference to a stored, code-computed fact. Any question the evidence base could not answer at the as-of date is stated as unanswered rather than softened.

What it says it may get wrong

  • Cost of equity has no filed provenance. Accepted from review: the risk-free rate, ERP and beta are sourced and dated but remain assumptions, and they dominate the residual-income output. The valuation section must publish implied P/TBV across a range of costs of equity; any single-point 'justified multiple' conclusion is not supported by evidence and must not be stated as one.
  • The 10-K/10-Q rate-shock simulation is a management model output whose deposit-beta, repricing and balance-growth assumptions are undisclosed and not reproducible. Accepted from review: it is labelled as such, not treated as regulator-verified, and is presented next to realised deposit betas computed by code from the quarterly average-balance tables against observed policy-rate moves.
  • Regulatory-source vintage risk. The 2026-06-30 UBPR cycle may not be published by 2026-09-12; a 2026 DFAST covering MTB may not exist if the firm is on a biennial cycle; the 2026 DEF 14A may post-date the run. Each source gets an availability check recording published vintage and retrieval date, and the fallback is to use the latest common published cycle for MTB and peers together rather than mixing periods inside one comparison.
  • Point-in-time cutoff: the quarter ending 2026-09-30 is incomplete, so the most recent reported period is 2026-06-30, leaving roughly ten weeks of unobserved credit and deposit behaviour. Questions about current-quarter margin direction are unanswerable from evidence.
  • Section-contract mismatch on valuation: a free-cash-flow DCF is not a valid frame for a bank holding company. The intended method is residual income on tangible common equity with a dividend-discount cross-check and an implied-versus-traded P/TBV comparison. If valuation_dcf requires FCF and WACC fields, the mismatch must be surfaced, not filled with inapplicable inputs.
  • CRE granularity risk: the split by property type (office, multifamily, retail, healthcare, construction), by geography, and by criticized or nonaccrual status within property type may appear only in investor supplements or call commentary rather than machine-extractable tables. Reserve coverage by property type may not be disclosed at all.
  • Reserve adequacy is not verifiable from public filings. CECL allowances rest on internal models, scenario weights and qualitative overlays that are described but not reproducible. The analysis can report coverage ratios and migration trends; it cannot conclude that reserves are sufficient.
  • Residual peer non-comparability. Even on common FR Y-9C lines, treatment of owner-occupied CRE, loans held for sale and construction can differ by filer practice, and tangible common equity from peer 10-Qs uses each filer's own intangible definitions. Any surviving difference must be stated in industry_landscape. Separately, the historical P/TBV series spans different rate regimes, so a multiple above or below its own history is context, not a valuation conclusion.
  • Restatement and amendment risk: no 10-K/A, revised regulatory report or reclassification filed after 2026-09-12 may be used, so figures may later prove superseded. FR Y-9C and Call Reports are routinely amended and the point-in-time vintage must be pinned per source.
  • Purchase-accounting accretion from prior acquisitions can inflate reported margin without representing durable spread. If accretion is not separately disclosed for every quarter in the window, the earnings-quality adjustment cannot be completed and the margin-peak conclusion weakens accordingly. Segment redefinition over the period may likewise prevent clean attribution of CRE exposure to a reportable segment.
