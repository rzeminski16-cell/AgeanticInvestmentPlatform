### Recorded Caveats

- Book equity was used as the equity weight because no market capitalisation was available. Book value is what the shares were issued and retained for rather than what they are worth, and for a profitable company it is usually far lower — so the equity weight is understated, the debt weight overstated, and the resulting WACC too low. Every valuation discounted at it is correspondingly too high.
- More than three quarters of the enterprise value is terminal value. The explicit forecast is contributing little, so the answer rests almost entirely on the terminal assumption rather than on the projected years a reader can check.
- The two terminal methods disagree by more than a quarter. That is information, not an error: they are two different guesses about the same unknowable quantity, and the distance between them is the honest width of the answer.
- The bridge from enterprise value to equity value is net debt alone. Associate holdings, minority interests and pension deficits are not read off the filings by this build, so a business carrying material non-operating items is valued as though it does not.

### Commentary

The recorded equity value is $287.5bn, against an enterprise value of $311.4bn before obligations are deducted. It should be read as one point on a wide band, and the band is structural rather than presentational: the build carries two terminal-value conventions, a growth-based terminal value of $505.8bn and an exit-multiple terminal value of $371.2bn. Terminal value carries the bulk of the present value under either, so the convention chosen, not the operating analysis, decides the answer. A reader who prefers the exit-multiple convention should carry a materially lower figure through every conclusion here, and we would not argue against them. The internal consistency checks frame the same span from the other side: implied terminal growth of 1.07% and an implied exit multiple of 19.1x, one of which will look generous depending on which convention the reader accepts.

The discount rate does most of the remaining work. Cost of equity is 6.35%, built on a beta of 0.28; cost of debt is 5.65% pre-tax and 4.52% after tax; the weights are 62.2% equity and 37.8% debt. Set against that, the company's own disclosure runs higher: it states a post-tax weighted average cost of capital used where recoverable value is assessed by reference to a market participant, and tests intangible assets under value in use at risk-adjusted post-tax rates reaching above that level. Both sit above the blended cost of capital assembled here, whose equity leg reflects a beta well below one. Discounting a research-based pharmaceutical group at a rate below the one the issuer applies to its own asset cash flows is a directional bias, not a symmetric uncertainty, and because terminal value dominates, the bias is amplified in the equity figure rather than damped. On this point we side with the sceptical reading: the equity value is an upper bound, not a central case.

The cash flow line deserves the same scepticism. Forecast free cash flow of $20.5bn sits close beneath forecast EBITDA of $26.5bn, and well above what the business actually converted in FY2025, when operating cash flow was $14.58bn before capital expenditure of $2.81bn. The wedge between the depreciation intensity and capex intensity assumptions is what creates the difference: depreciation is allowed to run while capital spending is held at a fraction of it. For a going-concern pharmaceutical business that shape describes an amortisation run-off rather than a plan, and it sits awkwardly beside a compound revenue growth assumption of 9.05% — growth of that order has to be funded through the very capex line the forecast suppresses. After the discount rate, this is the most optimistic input in the build, and correcting it moves value down, not sideways.

What the valuation ultimately turns on is stated plainly in the company's own accounting disclosure, if not in the arithmetic: the key estimates behind its intangible values are the outcome of R&D activities, the probability of technical and regulatory success, market volume, share and pricing used to derive peak-year sales, and sales erosion curves after patent expiry. Those variables decide whether FY2025 revenue of $58.7bn and operating income of $13.7bn compound or erode, and none of them is captured by a single growth rate and a terminal multiple. A recent late-stage combination readout in HR-positive breast cancer reported a safety profile consistent with the known profile of each medicine with no new safety concerns identified, which is supportive at the margin and no more than that. The build does not separate value between marketed products and the pipeline, so this note attributes no share of value to either and offers no implied pipeline figure; a split asserted without a supporting decomposition would be decoration.

Our judgement, stated as judgement: the recorded equity value should be treated as the top of a range whose lower end is anchored by the exit-multiple terminal value of $371.2bn. Re-struck at a discount rate consistent with the company's own disclosed impairment rates, and at a capital intensity consistent with what the group actually spent in FY2025, the value would be materially lower than the headline figure. Readers should take the operating conclusions here — strong cash conversion, heavy research spend, dependence on trial and patent outcomes — as better supported than the point value.
