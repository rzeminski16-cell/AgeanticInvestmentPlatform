## Segment Analysis

### Commentary

Microsoft reported fiscal year 2026 (year ended June 30, 2026) results under its three-segment structure: Productivity and Business Processes, Intelligent Cloud, and More Personal Computing. Productivity and Business Processes revenue and operating income both grew strongly, with gross margin expansion attributed to growth in Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud and efficiency gains, partially offset by continued AI infrastructure investment, with a favorable foreign-currency effect also aiding margins. Intelligent Cloud revenue rose substantially, led by Azure and other cloud services, while operating income grew more slowly than revenue as cost of revenue rose sharply on AI-infrastructure build-out, compressing gross margin percentage even as gross margin dollars increased; server products revenue was roughly flat while enterprise and partner services grew modestly. More Personal Computing showed the weakest dynamics: cost of revenue fell on lower hardware sales, gross margin improved on a mix shift toward higher-margin Search advertising and Windows OEM, but operating income growth was minimal because operating expenses rose on an Xbox-related impairment and continued research and development investment. Taken together, the mix shows Intelligent Cloud as the primary growth and capital-intensity driver, Productivity and Business Processes as the most margin-resilient segment, and More Personal Computing as the slowest-growing and least capital-intensive of the three this period, though a hardware impairment weighed on its reported profitability. At the consolidated level, gross margin, operating margin and net margin were 67.9%, 46.8% and 40.3% respectively for fiscal year 2026, and EBITDA margin was 58.5%, underscoring an overall highly profitable mix even as AI infrastructure spending compresses cloud-related gross margin percentages. A material structural change should be flagged: on September 2, 2026, Microsoft furnished, rather than filed, an Exhibit 99.1 presentation announcing a move to two reportable segments, Agents and Infra and Devices and Consumer, effective for fiscal year 2027 reporting. No recast of fiscal year 2026 or prior-period segment revenue or operating income onto the new structure has been published, and the furnished materials do not constitute audited financial statements. Consequently, this analysis is built entirely on the pre-change, three-segment basis; a like-for-like bridge between the two structures cannot be constructed from what has been disclosed, and future segment commentary will need to stand on its own basis rather than be compared directly against the figures above. Investors should also note that Azure and Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud, cited above, are management-defined cloud aggregates nested within reportable segments, not standalone reportable segments themselves, and are not separately audited GAAP line items.

## Earnings Quality

### Commentary

Reported profit for the fiscal year ended June 2026 is well covered by cash at the operating line. Operating cash flow of $182.9 billion ran ahead of net income of $133.7 billion, the pattern expected where deferred revenue and non-cash charges precede accrual profit. Share-based compensation of $12.4 billion is the largest such add-back and remains a genuine, recurring cost of labour; $22.3 billion of share repurchases sits in financing, so the same economic cost surfaces in two statements. Below the operating line the picture inverts. Capital expenditure of $115.9 billion left free cash flow of $67.0 billion, materially below reported net income. This is the central earnings-quality issue: an infrastructure build of this scale is charged to profit through depreciation over later years, so current margins carry the depreciation of a smaller historical asset base while the cash cost of the new base has already been paid. EBITDA of $194.2 billion against operating income of $155.2 billion marks the depreciation and amortisation wedge, and its future path depends on useful-life assumptions that the available disclosure does not set out. The property and equipment note, stated depreciable lives and any change in estimate, and lease commitments including leases signed but not yet commenced, are not in the material available, so the question of whether margin strength coincides with lengthened lives, rising capitalisation or assets not yet placed in service remains open, and no peer useful-life benchmark can be set against it. Gross margin of 67.9%, operating margin of 46.8%, EBITDA margin of 58.5% and net margin of 40.3% should be read as untested against that sensitivity. Where policy is disclosed, it reads conservatively. Internal software development costs are expensed until technological feasibility, which the company states is reached only shortly before products are released to production, leaving little scope for shifting development spend onto the balance sheet; research and development of $35.6 billion was borne in the period. Goodwill testing, by contrast, rests on discounted cash flow judgment: forecast cash flows, long-term growth and weighted average cost of capital are the softest estimates in the accounts. Two further items temper the quality assessment. Receivables of $80.9 billion are large against revenue of $331.8 billion, and without prior-year balances the collection trend cannot be judged here. And the company remains under IRS audit, having received notices of proposed adjustment on intercompany transfer pricing covering tax years from the mid-2000s into the early 2010s and seeking a substantial additional payment plus penalties and interest, a contingency whose resolution may differ from amounts recorded.

## Balance Sheet & Liquidity

## Cash Flow Analysis

### Recorded Caveats

- Book equity was used as the equity weight because no market capitalisation was available. Book value is what the shares were issued and retained for rather than what they are worth, and for a profitable company it is usually far lower — so the equity weight is understated, the debt weight overstated, and the resulting WACC too low. Every valuation discounted at it is correspondingly too high.
- More than three quarters of the enterprise value is terminal value. The explicit forecast is contributing little, so the answer rests almost entirely on the terminal assumption rather than on the projected years a reader can check.
- The two terminal methods disagree by more than a quarter. That is information, not an error: they are two different guesses about the same unknowable quantity, and the distance between them is the honest width of the answer.
- The bridge from enterprise value to equity value is net debt alone. Associate holdings, minority interests and pension deficits are not read off the filings by this build, so a business carrying material non-operating items is valued as though it does not.

### Commentary

Read as a whole, the block above says that the value of this business is not settled by the discount rate. It is settled by how long above-average growth persists, by whether the EBIT margin survives the reinvestment now running through the accounts, and by where capital intensity lands once the current build-out matures. The two terminal treatments are the fork: the closer the value per share produced by the Gordon growth and exit multiple closings, the less the conclusion depends on terminal convention; the wider they sit, the more weight the terminal value share is carrying relative to the explicit forecast.

Start with the operating base the forecast fades from. In the fiscal year ended June 2026 the company turned revenue of $331.8 billion into operating income of $155.2 billion, an operating margin of 46.8 percent, with EBITDA of $194.2 billion. Return on invested capital of 27.1 percent — NOPAT of $125.1 billion against invested capital of $461.7 billion — is the figure that disciplines any fade assumption: a long fade implicitly assumes incremental capital keeps earning near that level, while a short one assumes competition or sheer scale drags it toward the cost of capital.

The reinvestment wedge is the most consequential input. Operating cash flow of $182.9 billion was struck against capital expenditure of $115.9 billion, leaving free cash flow of $67.0 billion. Capex intensity, depreciation intensity and working capital intensity therefore do more work here than the revenue growth line: at this scale a modest change in assumed capex intensity moves near-term free cash flow more than a comparable change in growth. Depreciation intensity lags capex intensity by construction while spending is rising, so an assumption that holds intensity flat while depreciation catches up compresses free cash flow before it recovers, and the value effect depends entirely on how quickly intensity is allowed to normalise. Working capital intensity cuts the other way: deferred revenue of $73.0 billion is customer-funded, and a forecast that grows the subscription base carries a cash benefit a receivables-led working capital assumption would miss.

On the discount rate, the WACC is assembled rather than observed — risk-free rate, equity risk premium and beta into a cost of equity, pre-tax and after-tax cost of debt, and the equity and debt weights. It should be read as a band of assumptions, not a finding. Balance-sheet structure narrows what that band can do: net debt of $19.4 billion, 0.10 times EBITDA, with interest cover of 50.9 times, implies a small debt weight, so the WACC sits close to the cost of equity and the cost-of-debt inputs barely move it. Beta and the equity risk premium are the dials worth stressing, and they interact with terminal growth: at the low end of a discount rate band the Gordon growth closing becomes acutely sensitive to terminal growth, which is exactly where the exit multiple closing is the better discipline.

Tax is the quiet input. Income tax expense of $32.2 billion on pre-tax income of $165.9 billion sets the starting point for the tax rate assumption; a terminal rate materially above that level removes value that no plausible growth assumption restores.

The company's own framing of these variables should not be assumed away. Management states it will continue to invest in capital expenditure to support growth in cloud offerings and in AI training and other infrastructure — capital intensity here is stated policy, not a cyclical accident. It also expects existing cash, short-term investments, operating cash flow and access to capital markets to fund operations, dividends, share repurchases, debt maturities and material capital expenditures for at least the next twelve months and thereafter, which supports the financing assumption implicit in the weights while saying nothing about returns on that spending. The filing's risk discussion adds that adoption, retention and effective monetisation of cloud and AI products determine whether revenue growth keeps pace with the associated cost, and that the timing or magnitude of growth may not match it. That is the fade assumption in the company's own words. The same filing notes that management itself applies a discounted cash flow method in goodwill testing and flags the judgment involved in forecasting cash flows, long-term growth and the weighted average cost of capital.

The figures above do not carry a solve for the assumptions embedded in prevailing valuation, so this reads the method's inputs against the operating record rather than as a verdict on mispricing. The honest conclusion is a range whose width is driven by capital intensity and fade duration far more than by the discount rate.

## Scenarios & Sensitivities

### Commentary

**What the cases turn on:** A reverse-DCF on Microsoft starts from what fiscal 2026 delivered: revenue of $331.8 billion, an operating margin of 46.8%, operating cash flow of $182.9 billion and, after capital expenditure of $115.9 billion, free cash flow of $67.0 billion. **Bear case:** Assets under construction enter service and carry full depreciation before contracted backlog converts into billed consumption. The company's own risk disclosure states that expanding vertically-integrated capabilities, including proprietary hardware, infrastructure and AI models, could increase its cost structure, reduce margins and expose it to operational risk, and that infrastructure capacity depends on permitted and buildable land, affordable energy, and servers including graphics processing units. On that path cloud operating margin settles below the 46.8% consolidated mark, free cash flow stays compressed against the $115.9 billion spend, and the equity is discounted at the upper end of any defensible rate band.

**Base case:** Utilisation catches the depreciation step. The filing describes cloud economics resting on scale: datacenters at lower cost per unit, aggregation of diverse customer, geographic and application demand that improves utilisation, and multi-tenancy that lowers maintenance labour. If that holds, margin absorbs the charge — the EBITDA margin of 58.5% leaves room — and free cash flow re-expands as capital spending growth moderates rather than as revenue accelerates.

**Bull case:** Backlog converts faster than new capacity begins depreciating in full, consolidated margin holds near 46.8%, returns stay near the 27.1% mark, and net debt of $19.4 billion leaves the balance sheet able to fund the build without dilution.

**The input that dominates:** Cloud operating margin after assets under construction are in service. It applies across the whole $331.8 billion revenue base, so a move there swamps the rest. The discount rate band ranks second. Useful-life shortening moves reported depreciation, and therefore diluted earnings of $17.95 per share, more than it moves cash. Backlog conversion pace changes timing, not terminal economics.

**Judged by direction, not outcome:** The steady-state cloud margin once the current build depreciates in full, and any revision to server useful lives, cannot resolve inside a twelve-month horizon. Both should be judged by the direction of travel in interim filings — quarterly gross margin, the capital expenditure line, disclosed lives — rather than by outcome. The discount rate band and the conversion pace of contracted backlog are observable sooner.
