## 1. Executive Summary

AstraZeneca ended FY2025 with Total Revenue of **$58,739m**, up 9% at actual rates and 8% at constant exchange rates (CER), comprising Product Sales of $55,573m (+9%), Alliance Revenue of $3,067m (+39%) and Collaboration Revenue of $99m (−89%). Reported EPS was $6.60 (+45%) and Core EPS $9.16 (+12%) ([FY2025 results 6-K, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/901832/000165495426001073/a3234s.htm)).

In H1 2026 Total Revenue was **$30,672m**, +9% actual / +6% CER, with Core EPS of $5.21 (+12% actual, +11% CER) and Reported EPS of $3.60. Growth was described by the company as "double-digit growth in Oncology and Rare Disease offsetting headwinds from Farxiga US loss of exclusivity and China volume-based procurement" ([H1 2026 6-K, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000901832/000110465926086846/azn-20260630x6k.htm)). FY2026 guidance was reconfirmed on 27 July 2026: Total Revenue to grow a mid-to-high single-digit percentage at CER, Core EPS a low double-digit percentage, Core Tax rate 18–22%, with a low single-digit percentage positive FX effect on revenue ([H1 2026 press release](https://www.astrazeneca.com/media-centre/press-releases/2026/h1-and-q2-2026-results.html)). Management reiterated the **$80bn Total Revenue ambition for 2030** ([Nasdaq release, 27 Jul 2026](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/astrazeneca-results-h1-and-q2-2026-2026-07-27)).

**Why I am Underweight.** My equity DCF, built on the company's own cash flow statements and its guidance, produces a base-case value of **$127 per share** versus $159.64 in the market. Probability-weighting bear ($71), base ($127) and bull ($203) at 25/50/25 gives **$132**, a −17.3% capital return and −15.2% total return including the intended $3.30 FY2026 dividend ([H1 2026 6-K summary](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/AZN/6-k-astrazeneca-plc-current-report-foreign-issuer-5b4d4295f64b.html)).

The disagreement between my DCF and the multiple is identifiable and is the crux of this note. At $159.64 the shares trade on **15.7x** my FY2026E Core EPS of $10.17 and **16.4x** trailing-twelve-month Core EPS of $9.71 (computed: $9.16 − $4.66 + $5.21, from the FY2025, H1 2025 and H1 2026 releases). In FY2025 that gap was $4,735m at the operating line (Core operating profit $18,478m versus Reported $13,743m — computed from the [FY2025 results presentation](https://www.astrazeneca.com/content/dam/az/PDF/2025/Q4-FY/Full-year-Q4-2025-results-presentation.pdf)). A DCF that charges the cash cost of business development cannot reach the multiple-implied value.

**Reverse-engineered, the market is paying for:** a cost of equity of **7.20%** (at 2.0% terminal growth), or a **3.90%** perpetual growth rate (at my 8.5% cost of equity), or a sustained operating cash-flow margin of **30.8%** of revenue against 24.8% delivered in FY2025 ($14,575m / $58,739m). ## 2. Investment Thesis

**1. The revenue mix is being rebuilt in public, and the transition is not free.** AstraZeneca is simultaneously absorbing the loss of exclusivity on its single largest product and scaling an oncology franchise that is now approaching half the group. In H1 2026, Oncology was $14.1bn, +15% CER and **46% of revenue**, while BioPharmaceuticals was $11.2bn, **−5% CER** and 36% of revenue, with Respiratory & Immunology +9%, CVRM **−12%** and Infectious Disease **−26%** ([H1 2026 press release](https://www.astrazeneca.com/media-centre/press-releases/2026/h1-and-q2-2026-results.html)). Weighting those sourced growth rates by those sourced revenue shares, Oncology contributed approximately **+6.9 percentage points** of group CER growth and BioPharmaceuticals subtracted approximately **1.8 points** — meaning oncology accounted for *more than the entirety* of group growth in the half. That is a narrower base of growth than the headline "+6%" suggests.

**2. Core EPS is a poor proxy for owner cash.** FY2025 Core operating profit of $18,478m (31% of revenue) compares with Reported operating profit of $13,743m (23%) ([FY2025 presentation](https://www.astrazeneca.com/content/dam/az/PDF/2025/Q4-FY/Full-year-Q4-2025-results-presentation.pdf)). Meanwhile the company spent $3,270m on tangible and software capex in FY2025 ([FY2025 RNS](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/astrazeneca--azn/final-results/9422484)) and had total net investment cash outflows of $7,225m ([FY2025 Annual Report extract](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/901832/000110465926019110/tm261135d3_ex99-1.pdf)) — implying roughly $3,955m of non-capex investing outflows (computed as $7,225m − $3,270m), largely intangible purchases, contingent consideration and acquisitions. In H1 2026 alone the company paid a $1,104m upfront to CSPC within purchases of intangibles, agreed a $600m upfront to Dizal and a $200m upfront to Sino Biopharmaceutical ([H1 2026 Investegate](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/astrazeneca--azn/half-year-financial-report/9687613); [Nasdaq release](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/astrazeneca-results-h1-and-q2-2026-2026-07-27)). This is a permanent cost of the business model, and it is invisible in Core EPS.

**3. Cash conversion has stepped down, not up.** Operating cash flow in H1 2026 was **$6,224m**, against $7,099m in H1 2025 — a fall of $875m attributed to working capital movement, higher tax paid and FX, partly offset by higher operating profit ([H1 2026 Investegate](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/astrazeneca--azn/half-year-financial-report/9687613)). Cash tax paid was $2,051m, equal to **31% of Reported profit before tax**, against a Reported effective tax rate of 17% and 24% cash-to-PBT in H1 2025 ([same](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/astrazeneca--azn/half-year-financial-report/9687613)). OCF as a percentage of revenue fell from 25.3% in H1 2025 to 20.3% in H1 2026 (computed).

**4. The 2030 ambition is doing heavy lifting in the price.** Getting from FY2025's $58,739m to $80,000m by 2030 requires a 6.38% revenue CAGR (computed) *through* the Farxiga, Brilinta and Soliris erosion. Management says it is "on track to deliver our $80bn Total Revenue ambition, which assumes successes and setbacks" ([H1 2026 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000901832/000110465926086846/azn-20260630x6k.htm)) — a formulation made immediately after the CARDIO-TTRansform failure, which is precisely one of the setbacks.

**5. What would make me wrong.** A 7.2% cost of equity (the market-implied figure) is defensible for an A1/A+ rated business ([H1 2026 6-K, Note 3](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000901832/000110465926086846/azn-20260630x6k.htm)). If business development is genuinely growth capital rather than maintenance spend — i.e. if the in-licensed assets deliver revenue not already in my forecast — then deducting it in full while also forecasting the growth it buys double-counts. A reader who takes either view reaches fair value at or above spot. I set out that grid in Section 14 rather than hiding it.

## 3. Business Overview

AstraZeneca PLC is a UK-incorporated, science-led biopharmaceutical company headquartered in Cambridge, UK, formerly Zeneca Group PLC, renamed in April 1999 and incorporated in 1992; it serves primary and specialty care physicians through distributors and local offices across the UK, the Americas, rest of Europe, Asia, Africa and Australasia ([Yahoo Finance company profile](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AZN/)).

**Listing structure.** A material change occurred inside the review period: "ordinary shares in our company began trading on the NYSE on the 2nd February [2026], resulting in a harmonised listing structure across exchanges in London, New York and Stockholm" ([FY2025 results, Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/astrazeneca-results-fy-and-q4-2025-2026-02-10)). The ADR facility has been closed, and Computershare acts for direct holders of NYSE-listed ordinary shares ([AstraZeneca IR FAQs](https://www.astrazeneca.com/investor-relations/faqs.html); [6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/901832/000110465926025559/tm268365d1_6k.htm)). The prior Nasdaq listings were withdrawn on Form 25 ([SEC Form 25](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/901832/000110465926008613/tm264197d1_25.htm)). **Practical consequence for a US investor: the NYSE line is now one ordinary share, not a fractional-ADR, so the quoted price and per-share statistics in this note are on the ordinary-share basis.**

**Scale.** FY2025 Total Revenue $58,739m; FY2024 $54,073m; FY2023 $45,811m ([FY2025 20-F Ex. 15.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/901832/000110465926019130/azn-20251231xex15d1.htm)). Two-year revenue CAGR: **13.2%** (computed). The company reported **16 blockbuster drugs** in 2025, with the five largest being Farxiga $8,492m, Tagrisso $7,254m, Imfinzi $6,063m, Ultomiris $4,718m and Calquence $3,518m ([FY2025 Annual Report extract](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/901832/000110465926019110/tm261135d3_ex99-1.pdf)).

**Revenue architecture.** Three lines: Product Sales (own-book), Alliance Revenue (partnered economics) and Collaboration Revenue (milestones). Product Revenue — Product Sales plus Alliance Revenue — was introduced as a subtotal effective 1 January 2025 ([Q1 2025 release](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/astrazenecas-q1-2025-financial-results-2025-04-29)). Alliance Revenue is now material and fast-growing: $3,067m in FY2025 (+38% CER), including $1,798m from Enhertu, $673m from Tezspire, $422m from Beyfortus and $77m from Datroway ([FY2025 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/901832/000110465926019130/azn-20251231x20f.htm); [Patent Expiries schedule 2025](https://www.astrazeneca.com/content/dam/az/Investor_Relations/annual-report-2025/pdf/AstraZeneca_Patent_Expiries_of_Key_Marketed_Products_2025.pdf)). **Geography.** US Product Sales were $23,444m in FY2025 (2024: $21,655m; 2023: $17,961m), +8% ([FY2025 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/901832/000110465926019130/azn-20251231x20f.htm)) — **42.2% of Product Sales and 39.9% of Total Revenue** (computed). US Oncology was $11,187m, +18% (2024: $9,510m), i.e. **47.7% of all US Product Sales** (computed). ## 5. Industry & Competitive Positioning

**Position.** AstraZeneca's competitive position is strongest where I can measure it: US Oncology grew 18% in FY2025 to $11,187m and Imfinzi grew 35% in the US ([FY2025 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/901832/000110465926019130/azn-20251231x20f.htm)), against a US market in which, per the company's own commentary, Medicare Part D redesign was a drag and formulary access required discounting. Growing an oncology book at that rate while conceding price is a genuine franchise signal.

**Partnering is structural, not opportunistic.** The FY2025 results presentation lists collaboration partners including Daiichi Sankyo (Enhertu, Datroway), Merck & Co. (Lynparza), HUTCHMED (Orpathys), Amgen (Tezspire) and Ionis (Wainua) ([FY2025 presentation](https://www.astrazeneca.com/content/dam/az/PDF/2025/Q4-FY/Full-year-Q4-2025-results-presentation.pdf)). In the twelve months to July 2026 the company added: an exclusive licence to Dizal's Zegfrovy (sunvozertinib), $600m upfront plus up to $900m milestones plus tiered royalties, expected to close in H2 2026; a licence from Sino Biopharmaceutical/Chia Tai Tianqing for respiratory candidate TQC3721, $200m upfront plus up to $1.9bn milestones; a $1,104m upfront to CSPC Pharmaceuticals; a Jacobio pan-KRAS licence (JAB-23E73); AbelZeta's 50% China rights to C-CAR031; and the acquisition of Modella AI ([H1 2026 releases](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/astrazeneca-results-h1-and-q2-2026-2026-07-27); [H1 2026 Investegate](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/astrazeneca--azn/half-year-financial-report/9687613); [FY2025 RNS](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/RNS/59563de3-5a71-4031-9f0b-61dfc61dc769.html); [Nasdaq FY2025](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/astrazeneca-results-fy-and-q4-2025-2026-02-10)). It also has AI-related agreements with Tempus and Pathos ("the largest multimodal foundation model in oncology"), CSPC for oral candidates, and Nucs AI for therapeutic radioconjugates ([Yahoo profile](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AZN/)).

**Direct competition I could source.** In CVRM, management was asked on the Q2 2026 call about "the oral PCSK9 data next year and how it compares to Merck's Lipfendra," and replied that data are expected in H1 2027 and that they "aim for a competitive profile versus Merck's compound" ([Q2 2026 call highlights](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/astrazeneca-plc-azn-q2-2026-190035336.html)). Synagis lost 35% of sales in FY2025 to AstraZeneca's own partnered Beyfortus ([FY2025 AR extract](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/901832/000110465926019110/tm261135d3_ex99-1.pdf)) — internal cannibalisation with a different economic split.

**China.** "China volume-based procurement" was named alongside Farxiga US LOE as one of the two H1 2026 headwinds ([H1 2026 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000901832/000110465926086846/azn-20260630x6k.htm)). Lynparza has faced "generic launches in China," and Symbicort faces "competition from triple therapy on the ICS/LABA class ... in China" ([Q3 2025 review](https://www.tradingview.com/news/zacks:a15d18bea094b:0-azn-q3-earnings-top-estimates-sales-rise-as-most-key-drugs-outperform/)). The company also disclosed in 2024 a Chinese appraisal opinion relating to Imfinzi and Imjudo carrying a potential "fine of between one and five times the amount of unpaid" duties ([FY2024 results announcement](https://www.astrazeneca.com/content/dam/az/PDF/2024/fy/Full-year-and-Q4-2024-results-announcement.pdf)). I could not source the current status or quantum of that matter.

### The US drug-pricing question (part 1 of 3 posed in the brief)

**What the filings say.** Three distinct pricing mechanisms appear in AstraZeneca's own disclosure:

1. **Medicare Part D redesign** is explicitly named as offsetting demand growth in the US for Tagrisso, Calquence and Strensiq ([FY2025 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/901832/000110465926019130/azn-20251231x20f.htm)).
2. **Gross margin dilution from Part D reimbursement.** In the FY2025 gross-margin bridge the company lists "Pricing adjustments, e.g. to sales reimbursed by the Medicare Part D programme in the US, diluted the Gross Margin," alongside partner profit-share recorded in cost of sales and royalty buyout expenses of $235m in Q4 ([FY2025 RNS](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/RNS/59563de3-5a71-4031-9f0b-61dfc61dc769.html)).
3. **Formulary discounting.** Calquence US growth in FY2025 was net of "discounts to secure preferential formulary placement" ([FY2025 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/901832/000110465926019130/azn-20251231x20f.htm)).

**What the margin has actually done.** Gross Margin (redefined from 1 January 2025 as Gross Profit ÷ Total Revenue) was **82% on both a Reported and Core basis for FY2025**, but **80% in Q4 2025, down 2 percentage points** ([FY2025 presentation](https://www.astrazeneca.com/content/dam/az/PDF/2025/Q4-FY/Full-year-Q4-2025-results-presentation.pdf); [FY2025 RNS on the definition change](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/RNS/59563de3-5a71-4031-9f0b-61dfc61dc769.html)). Management guided that it "anticipates a lower gross margin in the second half of the year due to seasonal demand patterns for lower margin medicines" ([Q2 2026 call](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/astrazeneca-plc-azn-q2-2026-190035336.html)) — attributing the decline to mix, not policy.

**What I could not source, and will not guess.** I have read no primary source on: most-favoured-nation pricing arrangements; any direct-to-consumer government purchasing platform; Section 232 pharmaceutical tariffs; the Medicare maximum fair prices applying to specific AstraZeneca medicines or their effective dates; or any company-specific agreement with the US administration. The only adjacent reference I found is a trade headline noting that the dapagliflozin market was "already reshaped by Medicare price negotiation and AstraZeneca's authorized generic strategy" ([Citeline, 8 Apr 2026](https://insights.citeline.com/generics-bulletin/products/generics/us-opens-farxiga-to-generics-after-pricing-overhaul-65ZXHKBFTVEYLFDKF2JQE2YTPA/)) — a secondary source, and I do not build on it.

**My answer, bounded by what I read.** Roughly **40% of Total Revenue is US-sourced** (computed: $23,444m ÷ $58,739m), and within that, **48% is oncology** — largely specialty, physician-administered or high-cost oral products of exactly the type Part D redesign and negotiation target. The measurable effect to date is modest and second-order: gross margin has moved from 82% to 80% in the most recent quarter disclosed, with the company attributing the move principally to royalty buyouts ($235m) and mix, and naming Part D as *a* dilutive factor without quantifying it. **I therefore model gross-margin pressure of roughly 100–150bp of revenue building over the forecast, embedded in my operating cash-flow margin assumption, and I flag explicitly that a hard MFN regime — which I could not source — is not in my base case and would sit in the bear case.** A reader who has read the policy documents should override this section.

**Verdict: improving, but the Core measure systematically overstates distributable earnings.**

**(a) The Core-to-Reported gap is narrowing, which is a genuine positive.**

Sources as per Section 7.1. The FY2025 improvement is explicitly attributed to "a lower intangible impairment impact than in 2024" ([FY2025 AR extract](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/901832/000110465926019110/tm261135d3_ex99-1.pdf)). Over three years, roughly **$15bn of cumulative pre-Core charges** were excluded from the headline earnings measure.

**(b) Cash conversion was excellent in FY2025 and poor in H1 2026.**

- FY2025: OCF $14,575m ÷ Core operating profit $18,478m = **78.9%** (computed). OCF ÷ approximate Core net income ($9.16 × 1,551m = $14,207m) = **1.03x** (computed). OCF ÷ approximate Reported net income ($6.60 × 1,551m = $10,236m) = **1.42x**.
- H1 2026: OCF $6,224m ÷ Core operating profit $10.5bn = **59%** (computed). OCF fell $875m year on year while Core operating profit rose 11% ([H1 2026 Investegate](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/astrazeneca--azn/half-year-financial-report/9687613); [H1 2026 press release](https://www.astrazeneca.com/media-centre/press-releases/2026/h1-and-q2-2026-results.html)).

The company's own explanation: "Movement in working capital and short-term provisions, higher taxation paid and foreign exchange fluctuations, offset by increased Operating profit" ([H1 2026 Investegate](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/astrazeneca--azn/half-year-financial-report/9687613)). I treat this as timing-plus, not timing-only, because working capital is structurally negative: working capital at 30 June 2026 was **−$3,588m**, with a current ratio of 0.89x and a quick ratio of 0.67x ([Dukascopy analysis of H1 2026 results](https://www.dukascopy.com/swiss/english/marketwatch/market-news/Stock-Analysis/155557) — a secondary source; see Section 18 for my reservations about it).

**(c) Cash tax is running well above the book rate.** H1 2026 cash tax paid $2,051m = **31% of Reported profit before tax**, against a Reported effective rate of 17% and a Core rate of 18%; the H1 2025 comparison was 24% cash-to-PBT on an 18% book rate ([H1 2026 Investegate](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/astrazeneca--azn/half-year-financial-report/9687613)). **A 13-point gap between cash tax and the P&L rate is the single item in the accounts I would most want management to explain.** Full-year cash tax was $2,193m for 9M 2025 ([9M 2025 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/901832/000165495425012630/a4030g.htm)) and $2,750m for FY2024 ([FY2024 AR extract](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/901832/000110465925014732/tm251208d3_ex99-1.pdf)), so the H1 2026 figure is running at an elevated annualised pace.

**(d) One-off revenue has already washed out.** Collaboration Revenue of $923m in FY2024 (including a $600m Lynparza milestone from Merck) fell to $99m in FY2025 with $nil from Lynparza ([FY2025 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/901832/000165495426001073/a3234s.htm); [FY2025 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/901832/000110465926019130/azn-20251231x20f.htm)). **This is a quality improvement, not a deterioration: the FY2025 +8% CER growth was achieved despite an ~1.5pp headwind from the milestone reversal** (computed: $824m ÷ $54,073m).

**(e) Royalty buyouts sit in cost of sales.** In Q4 2025 AstraZeneca paid BMS $170m to reduce ex-US Saphnelo royalties to zero and Compugen $65m for part of its rilvegostomig royalty interest, both expensed in cost of sales, with $235m of royalty buyouts cited as diluting Q4 gross margin ([Nasdaq FY2025 release](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/astrazeneca-results-fy-and-q4-2025-2026-02-10); [FY2025 RNS](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/RNS/59563de3-5a71-4031-9f0b-61dfc61dc769.html)). These are economically capital transactions run through the margin — **conservative accounting, and I credit it.**

## 13. Valuation — Discounted Cash Flow

### 13.1 Method and why

I run an **equity-side (levered) DCF**, discounting free cash flow to equity at a cost of equity, and I do **not** subtract net debt at the end. Reasons: (i) I can source every operating-cash-flow input directly from the filings, whereas I could not source a depreciation-of-PP&E figure needed to rebuild an unlevered EBIT-based FCFF cleanly; (ii) interest is already inside operating cash flow as reported by the company; (iii) it avoids double-counting the amortisation add-back against business-development cash spend. The implicit assumption is that gross debt is rolled at a broadly constant level, which is consistent with A1/A+ ratings and the observed 2024–2026 net-debt range of $22.5bn–$26.9bn.

**FCFE = Operating cash flow − capital expenditure − business-development / intangible purchases.**

### 13.3 Cost of capital — honest construction

**What I could source:** AstraZeneca Finance USD notes maturing 2029–2034 carry coupons of **4.85%, 4.9%, 4.9%, 4.875% and 5.0%** ([FY2025 RNS](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/RNS/59563de3-5a71-4031-9f0b-61dfc61dc769.html)). **What I could not source: a risk-free rate, an equity risk premium, or a beta.** I therefore do not pretend to a CAPM build. Instead:

- Marginal pre-tax cost of debt, from the company's own recent coupons: **4.9%** (midpoint of 4.85%–5.0%).
- After-tax at a 20% tax rate (midpoint of guided 18–22%): **3.92%**.
- **Cost of equity: 8.5% — an assumption, not a derivation.** It sits **3.6 percentage points above the company's own observed long-dated debt cost**, which I regard as a reasonable equity premium for an A-rated, patent-exposed pharmaceutical. I flag it as the weakest link in the note.

- E = $247,596m (90.2% of capital); D = $26,912m net debt (9.8%)
- WACC = (0.902 × 8.5%) + (0.098 × 3.92%) = 7.67% + 0.38% = **8.05%**

## 15. Key Risks

**To the Underweight (i.e. risks that I am wrong):**

1. **Pipeline conversion.** More than 20 high-value readouts are due in the next 18 months, against a track record of 16 positive Phase III readouts and 43 approvals in 2025, and six positive Phase III programmes and eight first approvals in H1 2026 alone ([H1 2026 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000901832/000110465926086846/azn-20260630x6k.htm); [FY2025 RNS](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/astrazeneca--azn/final-results/9422484)). A cluster of positives would reset the growth fade in my model.
2. **Cost of equity.** At 7.20% the shares are fairly valued today. If long rates fall, my 8.5% assumption becomes the binding error.
3. **Operating leverage.** Core SG&A grew only 3% CER in FY2025 against 8% revenue growth ([FY2025 presentation](https://www.astrazeneca.com/content/dam/az/PDF/2025/Q4-FY/Full-year-Q4-2025-results-presentation.pdf)); H1 2026 Core operating margin reached 34.2% (computed). If that persists, my 26% terminal OCF margin is too low.
4. **H1 2026 cash flow was timing.** If the $875m OCF shortfall reverses in H2, FY2026 OCF exceeds my $14.4bn and the whole forecast shifts up.

**To the business:**

5. **Patent cliff beyond my visibility.** I could not read the per-product expiry schedule (Section 12). The terminal value — 58% of my base-case equity value — assumes a 2.0% perpetual growth rate that implicitly presumes successful replacement of the entire current portfolio. **This is the largest unquantified risk in the note.**
6. **Clinical failure.** CARDIO-TTRansform demonstrated the mechanism: on 9 July 2026 Wainua (eplontersen) failed its primary endpoint of CV mortality and recurrent CV clinical events to 140 weeks in ATTR-CM ([AstraZeneca press release](https://www.astrazeneca.com/media-centre/press-releases/2026/update-cardio-ttransform-phase-iii-trial.html)), and the shares fell around 9–10% in London and Stockholm — "their largest single-day decline since late 2024" ([Yahoo/Reuters](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/astrazeneca-shares-slide-heart-drug-090753497.html); [PharmExec](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/astrazeneca-shares-drop-following-wainua-failed-heart-disease-trial); [GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/8950916/astrazeneca-azn-shares-plunge-following-trial-setback)). Commentary raised "questions about the company's clinical trial design capabilities" ([PharmExec](https://www.pharmexec.com/view/astrazeneca-shares-drop-following-wainua-failed-heart-disease-trial)). A prespecified subgroup showed nominally significant results in monotherapy patients, with no effect in those on a stabiliser ([AstraZeneca](https://www.astrazeneca.com/media-centre/press-releases/2026/update-cardio-ttransform-phase-iii-trial.html); [Yahoo](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/astrazeneca-shares-slide-heart-drug-090753497.html)).
7. **US pricing policy — unquantified.** See Section 5. Part D redesign is already named as a drag on Tagrisso, Calquence and Strensiq US sales and as diluting gross margin ([FY2025 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/901832/000110465926019130/azn-20251231x20f.htm); [FY2025 RNS](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/RNS/59563de3-5a71-4031-9f0b-61dfc61dc769.html)). I could not source the current MFN/tariff position.
8. **China.** Volume-based procurement named as an H1 2026 headwind ([H1 2026 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000901832/000110465926086846/azn-20260630x6k.htm)); generic Lynparza launches and triple-therapy competition in the ICS/LABA class ([Q3 2025 review](https://www.tradingview.com/news/zacks:a15d18bea094b:0-azn-q3-earnings-top-estimates-sales-rise-as-most-key-drugs-outperform/)); an unresolved Imfinzi/Imjudo appraisal matter with a potential fine of one to five times unpaid amounts ([FY2024 results](https://www.astrazeneca.com/content/dam/az/PDF/2024/fy/Full-year-and-Q4-2024-results-announcement.pdf)) — quantum and status not sourced.
9. **Partner dependence.** Enhertu (Daiichi Sankyo), Tezspire (Amgen), Beyfortus (Sanofi), Lynparza (Merck), Wainua (Ionis) are material. AstraZeneca books 55.7% of combined Enhertu sales (computed). Partner-side decisions on pricing, supply or strategy are outside management's control.
10. **Impairment recurrence.** $1,065m of intangible impairments in R&D in FY2024, plus $504m in SG&A ([FY2024 AR extract](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/901832/000110465925014732/tm251208d3_ex99-1.pdf)), against $58,904m of goodwill and intangibles at 30 June 2026, equal to 117% of equity.
11. **Cash tax.** 31% of Reported PBT in H1 2026 versus a 17% book rate ([H1 2026 Investegate](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/astrazeneca--azn/half-year-financial-report/9687613)). If this is structural rather than timing, my OCF margins are too high.
12. **Working capital.** Negative $3,588m with a current ratio of 0.89x at 30 June 2026 ([Dukascopy](https://www.dukascopy.com/swiss/english/marketwatch/market-news/Stock-Analysis/155557)) leaves little cushion for a revenue disappointment.
13. **FX.** Guidance is set at CER with a separate FX overlay; H1 2026 actual growth (+9%) exceeded CER growth (+6%) by 3 points ([H1 2026 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000901832/000110465926086846/azn-20260630x6k.htm)). That tailwind reverses.

Self-consistent.
