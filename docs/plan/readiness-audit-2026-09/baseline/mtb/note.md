**3. Both the expense line and the revenue line are now non-comparable with 2025, and mortgage banking revenue acquires mark-to-market volatility. Management stated the accompanying fee and expense guidance changes have "**minimal impact on net income or PPNR**" [S14] — which is reassuring for the bottom line but means headline efficiency ratios flatter.

**4. The general ledger conversion restated prior rate disclosures.** M&T "refined our methodology for calculating annualized taxable equivalent rates for earning assets and interest-bearing liabilities. **Previously reported amounts have been adjusted to conform**" [S15][S6]. Changing the denominator of your yield disclosures in the same quarter you print a record margin is not an accusation of anything, but it deserves a flag.

**5. One clear positive: the provision exceeded charge-offs.** Q2 provision was **$120m** [S1] against computed net charge-offs of **$81m** — a **$39m reserve build** (computed). The allowance ratio nonetheless slipped 1bp to 1.52% because loans grew faster [S3]. Building reserves in a record quarter is the right behaviour.

**Net:** the operating improvement is real but the reported EPS trajectory overstates it by roughly 9 percentage points a year.

Tangible common equity to tangible assets fell **19bp sequentially and 60bp year-on-year** [S1] — *the release discloses the change but not the level, and I could not source the level.*

Management expects seasonally stronger 2H deposit growth to normalise short-term borrowings [S21]. That is the right plan; it has not happened yet.

Collateral capacity is deep: at 31 March 2026 approximately **$22.0bn of C&I, $13.6bn of CRE, $19.6bn of one-to-four family residential, $3.0bn of home equity and $15.1bn of other consumer loans were pledged** to secure FHLB and FRB New York borrowings and lines [S8].

Original January 2026 guidance was TE NII of **$7.27–7.35bn assuming 50bp of rate cuts and NIM in the low 370s** [S14]. Guidance has been walked down to the lower half of a range whose bottom was raised — and the rate assumption underpinning it has been invalidated.

### Is the net interest margin at a peak?

**Yes — management's own arithmetic says so.** NIM was 3.70% in both Q1 and Q2 2026 [S1][S17]. Full-year guidance of "high 3.60s" [S19] implies, if the full year lands at 3.68%, a **2H26 average of 3.66%**; at 3.67%, **3.64%** (both computed). That is a **4–6bp step-down from here**.

**Why?**

1. **Interest-free funds are contributing less.** M&T states explicitly that NIM was flat because "higher earning asset yields were offset by increased funding costs in support of loan growth and a **lower contribution from interest-free funds**" [S17].
2. **The funding mix is deteriorating.** Average deposits fell $0.7bn while average borrowings rose $4.0bn sequentially and $8.0bn year-on-year [S15][S4]. FHLB advances and senior notes cost far more than a 1.42% blended deposit rate [S17].
3. **Management is explicitly trading margin for volume** — "prioritising loan growth over margin preservation" [S26].

### What does deposit repricing do to it?

The relevant disclosed parameters are: **total deposit cost 1.42% (−1bp), interest-bearing deposit cost 1.95% (−2bps), cumulative deposit beta 56% since Q3 2024** [S17]; **loan yields 5.89% (+4bp)** [S20]; and an interest-bearing deposit base of **$120.9bn** with noninterest-bearing of **$44.2bn** at Q4 2025 [S14].

The loan-to-interest-bearing-deposit spread is **3.94pp** (computed) — extremely wide. That spread is what a hiking cycle attacks.

**Sensitivity (computed, on $195.3bn of average earning assets implied by $7.25bn NII ÷ 3.68%):**

| Fed move | Deposit cost at 56% beta | NIM impact, liability side only |
|---|---|---|
| +25bp | +$169m p.a. | **−9bp** |
| +50bp | +$339m p.a. | **−17bp** |
| +75bp | +$508m p.a. | **−26bp** |
| +25bp at 100% beta | +$302m p.a. | **−15bp** |

Against this sits the asset side. M&T's loan book reprices too — but only the floating portion, and M&T uses interest rate swaps to hedge a $141.4bn portfolio against rate changes [S32]. **I could not source M&T's disclosed asset-sensitivity or the notional and direction of its swap book, so I cannot net the two sides with precision. That is the largest single gap in this note.**

**The honest conclusion:** a symmetric 56% up-beta broadly offsets the asset-side benefit, which is why management guides to high-3.60s rather than expansion. My base case assumes **NIM of 3.62% in 2027 settling at 3.60%** — a 10bp compression from the Q2 peak, not a collapse.

**The macro is hostile to the guidance.** Fed funds are at **3.50–3.75%** after a 9–3 hold in July with three dissents *for a hike* [S36]; FedWatch put ~56% odds on a 25bp hike at the 16 September meeting [S38]; July FOMC minutes record the market "fully pricing in a 25 basis point hike by the September meeting and another one by the end of the first quarter of next year" [S35]; J.P. Morgan Wealth Management shifted its base case to a September hike, citing Iran-related energy supply shocks [S39]; and Chair Warsh said at Jackson Hole that underlying inflation had not "meaningfully improved" [S37]. The 10-year closed at **4.96% on 11 September**, its highest since 2023 [S33][S40].

### Volume growth

Loan growth is genuinely strong: Q2 average loans **+$3.0bn, the strongest quarterly growth since 2012** [S26], broad-based across commercial (+$2.3bn), CRE (+$57m average, +$1.1bn end-period), residential (+1%) and consumer (+2%) [S20][S15]. I assume **4% earning-asset growth** in the base case — below the Q2 annualised pace, above the 2025 trend.

Fee income has an identified catalyst: new sub-servicing relationships adding **~$35m of revenue in 2H26** [S19] at approximately **50% margin** on an FHA-focused business targeting a **$30–40m annual run-rate** [S23], plus continued strength in wealth management, trust, treasury management and capital markets [S19].

## 15. Key Risks

**1. Deposit repricing in a hiking cycle (high probability, high impact).** With a 56% cumulative deposit beta [S17] and $120.9bn of interest-bearing deposits [S14], each 25bp hike costs ~$169m a year, ~9bp of NIM (computed), before asset-side offset. The market prices a hike on 16 September and another by Q1 2027 [S35][S38]. **I could not source M&T's disclosed interest-rate sensitivity or swap book, so the net effect is genuinely uncertain — this is the biggest unquantified risk in the note.**

**2. Funding-mix deterioration (occurring now, medium impact).** Average deposits fell $0.7bn while borrowings rose $4.0bn sequentially and $8.0bn year-on-year [S15][S4]. LCR fell from 107% to 106% [S18][S1]. TCE/TA fell 60bp year-on-year [S1].

**3. CRE re-leveraging into a high-rate environment (medium probability, medium impact).** CRE grew $1.1bn in Q2 after years of deliberate reduction [S15], into a 4.96% 10-year [S33]. About 24% of office remains criticised [S16]. **Capital can absorb it** — $7.18bn of allowance plus CET1 headroom equals 29% of the CRE book (computed) — but earnings cannot absorb it without breaking the ROTCE that supports the multiple.

**4. Buyback at a premium to intrinsic value (occurring, medium impact).** 1H26 repurchases at $225.00 average, 1.92× TBVPS (computed), against my $221.70 intrinsic value.

**5. Earnings-quality opacity (medium probability, medium impact).** The BLG distribution amount is undisclosed [S1]; the MSR fair-value election makes 2026 non-comparable to 2025 [S1]; the general ledger conversion restated prior rate disclosures [S15].

**6. Capital normalisation (high probability, low impact).** CET1 fell 65bp in six months [S12][S1]. At the 10.0–10.2% target the buyback must shrink to roughly earnings less ~$770m of retention (computed) — plausibly halving the 2026 pace and removing ~4–5pp of annual EPS growth.

**7. Concentration and macro.** A Northeast/Mid-Atlantic footprint with limited exposure to high-growth Sun Belt markets, against peers building there [S43]. Energy-driven inflation from the Iran conflict is cited as a driver of the rate outlook [S39].

**8. Governance.** Combined Chair/CEO; average director age ~67 [S25]; CEO direct ownership reported at 0.09% by a third-party screen [S51] (unverified).

**Where I differ.** My $238 target sits **below** the Cantor ($285) and Argus ($280) bull cases by 16–17% and roughly in line with the $262 Hold and the $255 UBS target. It sits at the upper end of the Google Finance consensus range of $224–$265 [S31].

**The substantive disagreement** is with the bulls at $280–285, which on 146.2m shares implies **2.38–2.43× tangible book** (computed) and therefore, at a 10% cost of equity and 3% growth, a sustainable ROTCE of **19.7–20.0%** (computed). M&T has never reported a full year at that level in the periods I read: 14.54% in 2024 and 15.36% in 2025 [S11]. **To underwrite $285 you must believe that Q2 2026's 18.57% [S6] not only repeats but improves — while management guides 2H26 NII into the lower half of its range and expenses to the high end [S19].**

**Where I agree with the bulls:** credit quality is genuinely, durably better. Nine consecutive quarters of criticised-loan reduction [S22], a nonaccrual ratio at a two-decade low [S16], and 94% of criticised accrual loans current [S17] are not cosmetic. If my 40bp through-cycle charge-off assumption proves 8bp too conservative, that is worth ~$9 per share (from Grid 2).

**Where I agree with Truist's Hold downgrade and the caution around "rising funding costs and credit quality"** [S46]: the funding-mix deterioration in Q2 — deposits down, borrowings up $4.0bn [S15][S4] — is the clearest near-term negative in the disclosures.
