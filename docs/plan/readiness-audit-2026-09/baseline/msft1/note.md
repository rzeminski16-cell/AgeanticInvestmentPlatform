## 1. Executive Summary

Microsoft has just closed the strongest fiscal year in its history. FY26 revenue was **$331.8bn, up 18%** (up 16% in constant currency), operating income **$155.2bn, up 21%** (up 19% cc), net income **$133.7bn, up 31%** on a GAAP basis and up 22% on a non-GAAP basis, and diluted EPS **$17.95, up 32%** GAAP / up 22% non-GAAP [S1]. Microsoft Cloud revenue rose **27% to $214.4bn**, Azure and other cloud services rose **41%**, and commercial remaining performance obligation ("RPO") rose **84% to $678bn** [S2][S41]. Azure revenue passed **$100bn** for the first time and Microsoft 365 Copilot passed **30 million paid seats** [S37].

None of that is in dispute. The question this note addresses is price.

At $493.16 (close, 10 September 2026) [S17] against 7,425,545,491 shares outstanding at 23 July 2026 [S3], the market capitalisation is **$3,662bn** (my calculation: 493.16 × 7,425.5m), consistent with the $3.66–3.67tn reported by third parties [S18][S20][S22]. On FY26 GAAP EPS of $17.95 that is **27.5x** trailing earnings (my calculation), close to the 27.40x P/E reported by Robinhood [S18]. But trailing operating cash flow of **$182.94bn** against capital expenditure of **$115.95bn** leaves free cash flow of **$66.99bn** [S19], so the shares trade on roughly **54x trailing free cash flow** and an **FCF yield of 1.84%** (my calculations, using enterprise value of $3,642bn — see §13).

That yield must be set against a 10-year US Treasury at **4.95% on 11 September 2026**, the highest level since October 2023, with markets pricing roughly a **71% probability of a 25bp Fed rate *increase*** the following week [S23], and with Bloomberg reporting the 10-year "just below" 5%, approaching its highest since 2007 [S24]. This is the crux. Microsoft's operating performance is excellent; the discount rate applied to it has moved against the equity by roughly 90bp over twelve months [S23], and the cash conversion of that operating performance has deteriorated sharply.

My discounted cash flow, built entirely from sourced FY26 figures and explicitly stated assumptions, produces a base-case value of **$341/share at a 9.86% WACC** and **$407/share at an 8.87% WACC** (§13). The full 30-cell WACC × terminal-growth sensitivity grid sits below the current price at every combination except the extreme corner. My bull case — which requires Azure-led growth to hold above 15% for four more years and free cash flow to reach $369bn by FY36 — values the shares at **$488**, i.e. approximately the current price. **The market is paying the bull case.** I set the target at $405 (base case, 8.87% WACC, 3.0% terminal growth) for a total expected return of **−17.1%** including the $3.64 annualised dividend [S15], and initiate at Underweight.

**Three specific conclusions on the questions posed:**

1. **Cloud margin is not expanding; it is contracting, and capitalisation is masking how much.** Intelligent Cloud gross margin fell **419bp to 58.0%** in FY26, because segment cost of revenue rose 44% against revenue up 30% [S2][S5] (my calculations). Group operating margin *did* rise 116bp to 46.78%, but **every basis point of that came from operating-expense leverage**, not from cloud economics (§8).
2. **For the multiple to be justified, steady-state free cash flow must reach roughly $250bn — 3.7x the FY26 level — and must arrive far sooner than a normal capex-taper permits.** Alternatively, the market's implied WACC is **7.94%**, or its implied perpetual growth rate is **5.78%** (§13).
3. **Microsoft does not disclose an AI revenue line, so any "AI revenue" figure is an inference, not a fact.** What *is* disclosed: Microsoft Cloud added **$45.6bn** of the **$50.1bn** of total FY26 revenue growth — **91%** (my calculation from [S1][S2][S5]) — and Azure alone added roughly **$29bn** (my calculation from the disclosed >$100bn at +41% [S26]). Three years ago Azure's dollar revenue was not disclosed at all (§12).

## 2. Investment Thesis

**The thesis is not that Microsoft is a bad business. It is that Microsoft has become a capital-intensive business valued as though it were still a capital-light one.**

Four supporting pillars:

**(i) The cash conversion break is real and is not a timing artefact.** FY25 free cash flow was $136.2bn operating cash flow less $64.6bn of property and equipment additions = **$71.6bn**, or **25.4%** of revenue. FY26 free cash flow was $182.94bn less $115.95bn = **$66.99bn**, or **20.2%** of revenue [S19][S32] (my calculations). Free cash flow **fell 6.4% in a year when operating income rose 21%**. In the fourth quarter alone, free cash flow of **$19.64bn fell 23%** year on year [S25]. Capital expenditure reached **34.9% of revenue** [S33] (my calculation confirms: 115.95/331.839).

**(ii) The reported margin improvement is coming from the wrong place.** Group gross margin *fell* 88bp (68.82% → 67.94%) while opex/revenue fell 204bp (23.20% → 21.16%), netting to +116bp of operating margin (my calculations from [S5]). Operating expenses grew **7%** against revenue growth of **18%** [S2], with total headcount declining year on year in Q3 [S13] and a voluntary retirement programme running [S1]. Cost discipline is genuine and creditable — but it is finite, and it is being used to offset a deteriorating cloud gross margin, not to compound on top of an improving one.

**(iii) Reported profit growth is materially flattered by investment marks.** GAAP net income rose 31%; ex-OpenAI it rose 22% [S1]. FY26 included a **+$4,963m / +$0.67 EPS** net gain from OpenAI investments against a **−$3,620m / −$0.49** loss in FY25 [S1] — a $8.6bn swing in the year-on-year comparison from a single non-operating line. Q4 alone carried a **$3.2bn gain on the Anthropic investment** and $0.27 of discrete EPS benefit versus the April guide [S1]. Third-party analysis puts total non-operating investment gains in FY26 at about **$10.7bn** [S33]. These marks reverse in a drawdown.

**(iv) The obligation stack is growing faster than the disclosed balance sheet.** At 30 June 2026 Microsoft disclosed **$329.1bn of additional leases, primarily datacentres, that had not yet commenced**, with commencement dates spread from FY27 to FY33 [S29][S30]. That is **0.99x FY26 revenue** and **74% of book equity** (my calculations, using equity of $442.39bn [S36]). Separately, the FY27 useful-life change will push more future datacentre arrangements out of finance leases (which count in capex) and into operating leases (which do not) [S4] — improving the headline capex optic while the cash still leaves the building through operating cash flow.

**What would change my mind:** free cash flow inflecting above ~$120bn in FY27 on capex that plateaus rather than grows; Intelligent Cloud gross margin stabilising above 58%; a 10-year Treasury back below 4%; or credible disclosure of AI-specific revenue and unit economics that demonstrates gross margins on inference workloads approaching legacy Azure levels.

## 5. Industry & Competitive Positioning

Microsoft's own 10-K characterises its industry as "dynamic and highly competitive, with frequent changes in both technolog[y]…" [S41]. Rather than repeat generic hyperscaler commentary, I confine myself to what the filings and the tape actually evidence.

**Position of strength — the enterprise contract stack.** Commercial RPO of **$678bn, up 84%** [S1][S2] equals **2.04x FY26 revenue** (my calculation). No competitor disclosure I was able to source matches that ratio. Commercial bookings grew **18% excluding the impact from OpenAI** [S26].

**But the backlog has a concentration problem.** The 84% year-on-year increase cannot be reconciled to 18% ex-OpenAI bookings growth without a very large AI-model-developer commitment inside it. CNBC reports that RPO rose 8% sequentially and that **"sequential growth was driven by commitments from clients other than AI model developers"** [S25] — which, read carefully, tells you the *prior* quarters' step-up was driven by model developers. A backlog where a material slice is owed by a counterparty that Microsoft also holds an equity investment in, and whose marks run through Microsoft's own income statement [S1], is lower-quality backlog than a diversified enterprise book. I was unable to source the size of the OpenAI-related commitment and therefore do not quantify it.

**Position of strength — distribution of AI into installed base.** Microsoft 365 Copilot exceeded **30 million paid seats**, with net seat additions **more than doubling quarter over quarter** and user satisfaction scores doubling over the last three quarters [S26][S37]. Management is adding **usage-based billing alongside per-seat licensing for Copilot, Cowork and GitHub Copilot** [S26] — an expansion of addressable market, and simultaneously an admission that per-seat pricing alone was not capturing value.

**Position of weakness — capacity is the binding constraint, and it is capital, not code.** When demand exceeds capacity [S26] and two-thirds of spend is on short-lived CPUs and GPUs [S4], competitive advantage shifts toward whoever can finance and power silicon fastest. Microsoft's answer is to spend: **~$175bn of calendar-2026 capex and finance leases** [S4][S25], with FY27 capex to grow further, citing "demand signals across our portfolio" [S25]. Q4 capex included **the impact from higher component pricing** [S4] — evidence that Microsoft is a price-taker in the input market.

**Sentiment context.** On 11 September 2026, Oracle shares fell **5.38% to $152.94** [S17]; CoreWeave fell 6.13% to $89.12 [S17]. Adobe fell 2.37% to $248.83 [S17]. The AI-infrastructure complex was under pressure on the day of writing. Google announced a record **$15bn** AI infrastructure investment in Europe on 9 September 2026 [S40] — the capex race is not slowing.

## 8. Earnings Quality

This is where the note earns its keep, and where the first of the three posed questions is answered.

### Is the cloud margin expansion durable, or is capitalised infrastructure flattering it?

**The premise is wrong: cloud margin is not expanding.** Intelligent Cloud gross margin fell **420bp** in FY26 (62.2% → 58.0%), and Microsoft Cloud gross margin percentage fell from 69% in FY25 to 66–68% through FY26 [S9][S11][S12][S13]. Cloud is a *margin headwind* at Microsoft, and management says so in every quarterly performance release [S11][S12][S13].

**Group operating margin expansion is 100% opex leverage.** Decomposing the +116bp (my calculation from [S5]):

| Component | Contribution to operating margin |
|---|---|
| Gross margin % change (68.82% → 67.94%) | **−88bp** |
| Opex/revenue change (23.20% → 21.16%) | **+204bp** |
| **Net** | **+116bp** |

Operating expenses grew **7.4%** against revenue growth of 17.8%. Management attributes the restraint partly to genuine discipline — total headcount declined year on year in Q3 [S13] — and a voluntary retirement programme [S1]. **Opex leverage of this magnitude is not repeatable.** Opex/revenue cannot fall another 204bp annually from 21.2% without cutting into R&D, which grew because of "investments in research and development compute capacity, AI talent, and data" [S2].

**Yes, capitalisation is flattering the reported margin — and here is the arithmetic.**

- FY26 cash additions to property and equipment: **$115.95bn** [S19], confirmed by the 10-K's disclosure of a **$51.4bn increase** [S2] on FY25's $64.6bn [S32].
- Q4 FY26 depreciation, amortisation and other non-cash charges: **$11,022m** [S34], implying roughly **$44bn** annualised (my calculation).
- Accumulated depreciation on property and equipment rose from **$93,653m** at 30 June 2025 to **$111,723m** at 31 March 2026 [S7] — an increase of **$18.07bn in nine months**, or roughly **$24bn annualised** (my calculation).

So Microsoft spent **$115.95bn** of cash on productive assets and charged somewhere between **$24bn and $44bn** through the income statement. **The gap is $72bn to $92bn per year of economic cost not yet in the P&L.** Expensed as incurred, FY26 operating income of $155.2bn would be roughly **$63bn** and the operating margin roughly **19%** rather than 46.8% (my calculation: 155.237 − (115.95 − 44.1)). That is not the right accounting — these are genuinely long-lived assets — but it is the right *magnitude check* on how much of the 46.8% margin is a deferral.

**Three further quality flags:**

1. **The useful-life extension is a capex-optics change dressed as a depreciation change.** Extending datacentre and office building lives from 15 to 25 years reduces the annual depreciation rate on those assets from 6.67% to 4.0% (my calculation). Management says the operating income benefit in FY27 is "minimal" [S4][S38] — credible, since two-thirds of spend is short-lived GPUs/CPUs [S4] and the change is prospective. But the *disclosed* consequence is that "more of our future datacenter leases will shift from finance leases to operating leases… Finance leases are included in capital expenditures while operating leases are not," adjusting calendar-2026 capex expectation from approximately $190bn to **approximately $175bn** [S4]. Management explicitly confirms that "outside of this useful life impact, our calendar year 2026 CapEx investment expectations remain unchanged" [S4]. **$15bn of spending moved accounting bucket, not existence.** And operating-lease payments sit inside operating cash flow — Hood noted Q4 operating cash flow growth was "partially offset by an increase in operating lease payments" [S4]. So the shift reduces reported capex *and* reduces operating cash flow, leaving free cash flow roughly unchanged while the capex headline improves.
2. **Non-operating marks drove the GAAP/non-GAAP gap.** FY26 net income +31% GAAP versus +22% non-GAAP; the difference is a **$4,963m OpenAI gain (+$0.67 EPS)** in FY26 against a **$3,620m loss (−$0.49)** in FY25 [S1]. Q4 included a **$3.2bn Anthropic gain** [S1][S25]. Third-party analysis totals FY26 non-operating investment gains at ~$10.7bn [S33]. Ex-OpenAI FY26 EPS is **$17.28** (my calculation: 17.95 − 0.67), putting the trailing P/E at **28.5x** rather than 27.5x.
3. **An unexplained $22.2bn investing outflow.** The 10-K discloses a "$22.2 billion increase in cash used in other investing primarily to facilitate the purchase of components" [S2]. Microsoft is pre-funding or financing component supply on a scale that is roughly a third of FY26 free cash flow. I could not source further detail. It deserves a question on the next call.

**Working capital caution.** Short-term unearned revenue fell from **$64,555m** at 30 June 2025 to **$50,924m** at 31 March 2026 [S7], and accounts receivable from $69,905m to $60,041m [S7] — both seasonal, but the June-quarter unearned-revenue spike is the mechanism by which Q4 operating cash flow reached $55.4bn, +30% [S4]. Q4 free cash flow of $19.6bn on operating cash flow of $55.4bn is the honest quarterly picture.

## 12. Growth Outlook

### How much of the growth is AI revenue that did not exist three years ago?

**The honest answer begins with a disclosure gap: Microsoft does not report an AI revenue line.** There is no sourced figure for "Microsoft AI revenue" in the FY26 10-K, the Q4 press release, or the earnings call [S1][S2][S4]. Anyone quoting one is inferring. So am I, and I show the working.

**What is disclosed:**

| Metric | FY26 | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Azure and other cloud services revenue growth | **+41%** (FY), **+43%** (Q4) | [S2][S1] |
| Azure revenue level | **surpassed $100bn for the first time** | [S37][S26] |
| Microsoft Cloud revenue | **$214.4bn, +27%** | [S2][S41] |
| Microsoft 365 Copilot paid seats | **>30 million** | [S37][S26] |
| Commercial RPO | **$678bn, +84%** | [S1][S2] |

**My inferences, clearly labelled:**

1. **Azure added roughly $29bn of revenue in FY26 alone.** If Azure exceeded $100bn having grown 41% [S26][S37], FY25 Azure was approximately **$70.9bn** and the FY26 increment approximately **$29.1bn** (my calculation). Total group revenue increased **$50.1bn** (my calculation from [S5]). So Azure alone is roughly **58% of all FY26 revenue growth**.
2. **Microsoft Cloud accounted for 91% of FY26 revenue growth.** Microsoft Cloud at $214.4bn, +27% [S2] implies FY25 of **$168.8bn** and an increment of **$45.6bn** (my calculations) — against $50.1bn of total growth. Microsoft Cloud is now **65% of group revenue** (my calculation).
3. **Three years ago, none of these disclosures existed in this form.** Microsoft disclosed Azure *growth rates* but its **dollar revenue "surpassed $100 billion for the first time"** in FY26 [S37] — i.e. the level was not previously reported. I could not source FY2023 revenue at all (I was able to derive FY24 revenue of **$245.1bn** from the FY25 10-K's disclosed $36.6bn increase [S9], but not FY23). **I therefore cannot compute a three-year AI-revenue delta and will not manufacture one.**
4. **Copilot revenue cannot be sourced.** The 30 million paid seats figure is a seat count [S37]. Microsoft discloses neither realised price per seat nor Copilot revenue, and has just introduced **usage-based billing alongside per-seat licensing** [S26], which will make the seat count a still-weaker revenue proxy.

**My best-supported answer:** of Microsoft's $50.1bn of FY26 revenue growth, **$45.6bn (91%) came from Microsoft Cloud and approximately $29bn (58%) from Azure specifically**. A substantial but unquantifiable share of that Azure increment is AI inference and training workload that did not exist at scale three years ago — the company's own attribution of gross-margin compression to "continued investments in AI infrastructure and growing AI product usage" [S12][S13] confirms AI is now a large share of cloud cost of revenue, and cost of revenue is a reasonable proxy for workload mix. **But the specific dollar figure is not disclosed and I decline to estimate it.** What I will say is that the AI revenue is *lower-margin* than what it displaced: Intelligent Cloud gross margin fell 420bp while the AI revenue arrived (§8).

### 13.5 What would have to be true for the current multiple to be justified?

Three equivalent framings, all my calculations:

**(a) The discount rate would have to be 7.94%.** Holding my base-case free cash flow path constant, an enterprise value of $3,642bn requires a WACC of **7.94%** — implying an equity risk premium of approximately **3.0%** at a 4.95% risk-free rate and a beta of 1.00. That is a low ERP by historical standards, in a week when the 10-year Treasury reached its highest level since 2023 and the market priced a 71% chance of a rate *rise* [S23][S24].

**(b) Perpetual growth would have to be 5.78%.** At my 9.86% WACC, the terminal growth rate required to justify $3,642bn of EV on the base-case path is **5.78%** — i.e. Microsoft growing in perpetuity at above the nominal risk-free rate. That is not a sustainable assumption; it implies Microsoft eventually becomes the economy.

**(c) Free cash flow would have to reach ~$250bn, and reach it much sooner than a normal capex taper allows.** In a simple perpetuity at 9.86% WACC and 3.0% growth, the current EV requires steady-state free cash flow of **$250bn** — **3.7x** FY26's $66.99bn, a **14.1% ten-year CAGR** in free cash flow, and a **31.5% free cash flow margin** on my FY36 revenue of $792bn. My base case actually *gets* to $265bn of free cash flow by FY36 — but a decade of discounting destroys the arithmetic. **The market is paying today for a cash-flow profile that, on my assumptions, arrives in the mid-2030s.** For the price to be right, either the taper must be faster (capex falling to ~20% of revenue by FY29 rather than FY32), or growth must hold in the mid-teens for far longer, or both.

**Cross-check on multiples.** At $493.16 the shares trade on **27.5x** FY26 GAAP EPS, **28.5x** FY26 EPS excluding the $0.67 OpenAI gain, **25.7x** my FY27E, **11.0x** EV/revenue, **23.5x** EV/operating income and **54.4x** EV/free cash flow, for an FCF yield of **1.84%** — **311bp below** the 10-year Treasury (all my calculations). Even on a "normalised" free cash flow that charges only ~$44bn of annualised depreciation [S34] rather than $115.95bn of actual capex, giving FCF of **$138.9bn**, the multiple is **26.2x EV/FCF** for a **3.81%** yield — still **114bp below** the risk-free rate.

## 15. Key Risks

**Risks to my Underweight (i.e. why I could be wrong):**

1. **Rates.** The entire valuation case rests on a 4.95% risk-free rate [S23]. That rate has risen 88bp in a year [S23] amid an Iran conflict driving oil above $100 and accelerating August PPI [S23]. A ceasefire, an energy-price reversal and a dovish Fed pivot would compress the discount rate mechanically. At a 7.94% WACC my base case equals the market price (§13.5).
2. **Capacity-constrained demand is real, and capex could prove to be the highest-return spend in corporate history.** Management states demand exceeds available capacity [S26]. If so, every dollar of the ~$175bn calendar-2026 programme [S4] is pre-sold. My base case does not assume this.
3. **Copilot monetisation is a genuine option I have not valued separately.** 30m paid seats, net adds more than doubling sequentially, satisfaction scores doubling over three quarters, and a new usage-based billing motion [S26][S37]. Because Microsoft discloses no Copilot revenue, I have embedded it in the revenue line rather than valued it. If usage-based billing produces step-function revenue per user, my growth path is too low.
4. **The useful-life extension may be economically right.** If datacentres genuinely last 25 years [S4][S38], the depreciation drag I assume is overstated and the steady-state margin is higher.
5. **Positioning.** Consensus is "Strong Buy" with a $572.92 average target [S19] and Pershing Square added 6.2m shares on 8 September 2026 [S39]. Being short-of-consensus on a mega-cap momentum name is a painful place to be wrong.

**Risks to the business (i.e. why the bear case could happen):**

6. **Depreciation catch-up.** $115.95bn of FY26 capex [S19] against ~$24–44bn of depreciation charged [S34][S7] is a $72–92bn annual deferral (calc.). As the FY25–FY27 vintages fully enter the P&L, reported operating margin compresses independently of revenue. The Q4 FY26 exit margin of 45.1% versus Q1's 48.9% [S5] is the leading edge of this.
7. **RPO concentration.** Commercial RPO up 84% to $678bn [S1] against ex-OpenAI bookings up 18% [S26] implies heavy dependence on AI-model-developer commitments; CNBC's framing of sequential growth as driven by "clients other than AI model developers" [S25] confirms the composition. Counterparty stress at a model developer would hit revenue, backlog *and* Microsoft's equity marks simultaneously.
8. **Off-balance-sheet lease commitments.** $329.1bn of leases not yet commenced, commencing FY27–FY33 [S29][S30] — 74% of book equity (calc.). These become fixed costs regardless of utilisation.
9. **Component pricing and supply.** Q4 capex explicitly included "the impact from higher component pricing" [S4], and $22.2bn of incremental "other investing… to facilitate the purchase of components" [S2] suggests Microsoft is funding supply chain security. Both are margin and cash-flow risks.
10. **Legacy melt is accelerating and is guided.** Windows OEM and Devices high-teens decline, Server products and M365 Commercial products mid-single-digit declines, all in FY27 [S31]; Xbox content and services already −5% with Q4 impairment charges [S2][S1]. MPC revenue fell 1.1% in FY26 [S5].
11. **Litigation and regulatory.** A securities class action with an 11 August 2026 lead-plaintiff deadline [S39]; additional news-organisation lawsuits reported 8 September 2026 [S39]; and a disclosed Item 3 legal proceedings reference to Note 14 – Contingencies [S2]. **I could not source the substance of any of these and do not assess their merit or quantum.**
12. **Investment-mark volatility.** ~$10.7bn of FY26 non-operating gains [S33] against a $3,620m FY25 loss [S1]. This line alone swings EPS by more than a dollar year to year.
13. **Concentration of profit in one segment.** PBP is 54% of group operating income (calc. from [S5]) and depends on Microsoft 365 pricing power in a market where AI assistants are being bundled by every competitor.

### 18.2 Discrepancies I found and could not resolve

1. **Finance lease liabilities.** Common-size data implies long-term finance lease liabilities of **8.22% of the balance sheet ≈ $62bn** at FY26 [S35]; Yahoo reports "capital lease obligations" of **$16.53bn** [S36]. These cannot both be right. The FY26 10-K lease note would settle it. Immaterial to my valuation (§13.1) but material to any leverage assessment.
2. **FY26 shareholder returns.** Management said "over $43 billion" [S4]; a third party says **$48.7bn** [S33]; the nine-month cash flow statement showed $17.692bn of buybacks and $19.687bn of dividends [S32]. Different bases (declared vs paid). I used management's figure and did not reconcile.
3. **The ~$175bn capex figure.** The earnings call frames it as the **calendar-2026** expectation, revised down from ~$190bn purely because of the finance-to-operating-lease reclassification [S4]; one secondary source presents it as a **FY2027** figure [S28]. I used the call.
4. **FY26 balance sheet.** I could not retrieve the as-filed 30 June 2026 balance sheet from the 10-K or 8-K and relied on secondary sources (§9). **This is the weakest-sourced part of the note.**
5. **Q4 FY26 Microsoft Cloud gross margin percentage.** Sourced for Q1 (68%), Q2 (67%) and Q3 (66%) [S11][S12][S13] but not Q4. The company-level Q4 gross margin was 67%, down year on year [S26].
6. **FY2023 financials.** Not sourced, which is why the "three years ago" question in §12 is answered on a two-year (FY24→FY26) basis with the limitation stated.
7. **Whether Microsoft changed its reportable segments.** Suggested by an 8-K "Segment History as Reported" exhibit [S5] and by commentary on "improved cloud transparency" [S40], but I could not source the 8-K narrative (§3).

### 18.3 Where I disagree with management

- **On the useful-life change.** I accept the accounting judgement [S4][S38]. I disagree with the framing. Presenting a **$15bn reduction in headline capex guidance** that arises purely from reclassifying finance leases as operating leases [S4], in the same remarks as the depreciation-life change, invites the reading that capital intensity is easing. Management does say the underlying investment plan is unchanged [S4] — to their credit, and clearly. But the *number investors anchor on* went down while the spending did not.
- **On "free cash flow will remain positive."** For a company with $182.9bn of operating cash flow [S2], "positive" is guidance that carries almost no information. I read it as signalling that FY27 free cash flow could be materially below FY26's $66.99bn [S19].
- **On the RPO headline.** "+84% to $678bn" [S1] is a true statement and a misleading impression when bookings ex-OpenAI grew 18% [S26]. Management should disclose the model-developer share of RPO.

### 18.4 Where I disagree with the market

- **Consensus "Strong Buy", $572.92 average target** [S19], and Pershing Square adding 6.2m shares [S39], versus my Underweight and $405. My best account of their view: they are applying a lower ERP, assuming a faster capex taper, and valuing Copilot optionality explicitly. The single input on which the debate turns is the discount rate — and at 7.94% implied (§13.5), the market's is 192bp below mine.

### 18.5 Confidence statement

**High confidence:** FY26 income statement, segment detail, quarterly progression, operating and free cash flow, capex, share count, current price, dividend, risk-free rate. All read from primary or high-quality sources and internally cross-checked (§18.1).

**Moderate confidence:** the FY26 balance sheet (secondary sources only); the finance-lease and off-balance-sheet lease figures; my FY27E EPS of $19.19 (dependent on an assumed nil non-operating income).

**Low confidence / not established:** everything in §6 relating to the proxy statement; the entire peer multiple set in §17.2; any specific AI revenue figure; the equity risk premium and beta underlying the cost of capital — **these are assumptions, not measurements, and the valuation conclusion is explicitly sensitive to them** (§13.6, §14.2).

**Bottom line:** Microsoft is an exceptional operating business whose cash conversion has halved in margin terms in two years while its capital intensity has risen by 1,200bp of revenue, priced at a 1.84% free cash flow yield against a 4.95% risk-free rate. The base case is $341, the target is $405 at the favourable end of my cost-of-capital range, and the current price is my bull case. **Underweight.**
