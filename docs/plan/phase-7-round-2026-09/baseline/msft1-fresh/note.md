## 1. Executive Summary

- **The business is performing very well.** FY26 (year to 30 June 2026):
  - Revenue was $331.8bn, up 18%, and operating income was $155.2bn, up 21% [[3]][r3].
  - Azure passed $100bn of annual revenue, up 41% [[5]][r5].
  - Commercial remaining performance obligation (RPO, i.e. contracted revenue not yet recognised) rose 84% to $678bn [[4]][r4].
  - Microsoft 365 Copilot passed 30m paid seats [[4]][r4].
- **The price already assumes a lot.** At $500.59 the shares trade on 27.9x trailing GAAP EPS. The earnings yield is 3.6% ($17.95 / $500.59), below a 10-year Treasury yield of 5.10% [[30]][r30]. The FCF yield is 1.8%.
- **Valuation.** My base DCF uses a 9.55% WACC. That is built from a sourced risk-free rate plus an equity risk premium and beta that I had to assume (§13). It gives $433 per share today and $470 in 12 months. For the current price to be fair, one of these would roughly need to be true:
  - the discount rate is about 8.75%; or
  - ten-year revenue growth averages about 13.1% a year instead of 11.2%; or
  - the long-run EBIT margin is about 52.7% instead of 45%; or
  - capital spending is about 45% below my path.
- **Is the cloud margin expansion durable, or is capitalised infrastructure flattering it?** There is no cloud *gross* margin expansion to defend:
  - Microsoft Cloud gross margin fell from 68% in Q1 FY26 to 66% for the year [[43]][r43], and was 65% in Q4 [[5]][r5].
  - Intelligent Cloud segment gross margin fell from 62.2% to 58.0% (from segment tables [[6]][r6]).
  - Group operating margin still rose, from 45.6% to 46.8%, because operating expenses grew only 7% [[1]][r1] and Productivity & Business Processes margin widened.
  - Capitalisation does flatter the numbers. Capex was 3.4x depreciation. Payables for capex rose by $19.8bn. Servers are depreciated over up to six years. And a lease reclassification lowers the headline capex figure. I therefore expect gross margin to keep compressing.
- **How much of the growth is AI revenue that did not exist three years ago?** Microsoft's own disclosure is an AI revenue run-rate of over $37bn, up 123% [[10]][r10]:
  - That implies about $16.6bn a year earlier, so about $20.4bn of new annualised AI revenue.
  - That is roughly 41% of the $50.1bn FY26 revenue increase (a rough comparison, because it sets a run-rate against a fiscal year).
  - About 11% of revenue now comes from a category that barely existed before Microsoft 365 Copilot launched in November 2023 [[56]][r56].
- **Catalysts:** the Q1 FY27 results, the first under the new two-segment structure with Azure revenue disclosed in dollars [[60]][r60]; the AGM on 8 December 2026 [[2]][r2]; and the direction of interest rates.

## 2. Investment Thesis

**The bull points I accept:**

1. **Contracted demand.** Commercial RPO of $678bn is more than twice FY26 revenue [[55]][r55]. Management says customer demand "continues to exceed supply" [[5]][r5].
2. **Accelerating growth at scale.** Azure grew 43% in Q4 [[34]][r34], and Q1 FY27 guidance is about 45% in constant currency [[5]][r5]. Microsoft Cloud reached $214.4bn, up 27% [[20]][r20].
3. **Strong earnings and a solid balance sheet.** FY26 operating margin was 46.8%, which a third-party analysis calls the highest in five years [[43]][r43]. Net cash was $36.5bn (cash and short-term investments of $76.8bn less $40.3bn of debt [[3]][r3]).

**Why I rate it Hold rather than Buy:**

1. **Capital intensity has changed the economics.**
   - Capex was $115.9bn, or 34.9% of revenue, against 22.9% a year earlier [[43]][r43].
   - FCF fell to about $67.0bn (my calculation: $182.9bn operating cash flow [[1]][r1] − $115.9bn capex [[47]][r47]).
   - Third-party calculations put ROIC down from 29.3% in FY24 to 25.5% [[43]][r43].
2. **The cost of capital is higher.** The 10-year Treasury yield hit 5.10%, its highest since 2007 [[30]][r30]. The Fed has started a hiking cycle [[33]][r33].
3. **Reported margins understate the cost still to come.** Depreciation lags capex, and management has chosen long asset lives (§8).

### Key question 1: Is the cloud margin expansion durable, or is capitalised infrastructure flattering it?

**What the numbers show:**
- *Gross* margins in cloud are falling:
  - Microsoft Cloud gross margin: 68% in Q1, 66% for FY26 [[43]][r43], 66% in Q3 [[8]][r8], 65% in Q4 [[5]][r5].
  - Management attributes this to "sales mix shift to Azure" and AI infrastructure [[5]][r5].
  - IC segment gross margin fell 4.2 points in one year (62.2% to 58.0%), and 8.1 points over two years according to a third-party calculation [[43]][r43].
- *Operating* margin in IC held up much better (42.0% to 41.3%), and group operating margin rose. The reason is expense control:
  - IC operating expenses grew 6.7% against 29.7% revenue growth [[6]][r6].
  - Group operating expenses grew 7% [[1]][r1].
  - Headcount fell 2% year on year [[5]][r5].
- So the "expansion" comes from spreading fixed operating costs over faster revenue, plus a richer PBP mix. It does not come from the economics of the infrastructure itself.

**How capitalisation flatters the numbers:**
1. **Depreciation lags spending.** FY26 capex of $115.9bn was 3.38x depreciation of $34.3bn [[1]][r1]. Depreciation rose from $15.2bn in FY24 to $34.3bn [[1]][r1]. $34.6bn is committed to buildings still under construction [[1]][r1], and those assets are not yet being depreciated.
2. **Asset lives are long.** Servers and network equipment are depreciated over "two to six years" [[1]][r1]; the upper end was raised from four to six years in FY23 [[52]][r52]. Amazon went the other way, shortening some server lives from six to five years in February 2025 [[53]][r53].
   - Illustration using the Q3 gross balance of servers, network equipment and software ($190.9bn [[8]][r8]): moving from six-year to five-year lives adds about $6.4bn a year of depreciation (4.1% of FY26 EBIT). Moving to four years adds about $15.9bn (10.2% of EBIT).
   - This overstates the effect, because it includes software (three-year life) and assets already part-depreciated. It is only meant to show the scale.
3. **New change from FY27.** Datacentre and office building lives go from 15 to 25 years. Management says this gives a "minimal benefit to FY27 operating income". The bigger effect is that more datacentre leases become operating leases, which do not count as capex [[5]][r5]. That takes roughly $15bn off the calendar-2026 capex headline [[37]][r37].

**My view.** The operating margin is durable only as long as expense control can offset gross margin falling each year. A third-party model estimates a five-point depreciation drag by FY29 [[43]][r43]. My base case assumes EBIT margin falls to about 44.5% before recovering. The best evidence against my view is that management guides FY27 operating margin down by "less than a point" even as capex grows [[5]][r5].

## 5. Industry & Competitive Positioning

- **Size.** At more than $100bn, Azure is smaller than AWS but bigger than Google Cloud [[34]][r34].
- **Rivals are also growing fast:**
  - AWS re-accelerated to 37% growth [[41]][r41].
  - Google Cloud reportedly grew 82% in its June quarter [[62]][r62]. This comes from a secondary source; I did not verify it against Alphabet's filing.
- **Customer preference.** In a Jefferies survey of US CIOs, 55% named Azure as their primary cloud provider, against 28% for AWS [[29]][r29].
- **Industry capex is very large:**
  - Four large spenders together spent almost $172bn on capex in the June 2026 quarter [[64]][r64].
  - Amazon's trailing-twelve-month purchases of property and equipment were $173.0bn [[63]][r63].
  - AI companies have issued more than $1.5tn of corporate debt this year [[32]][r32].
- **Component costs.** About $25bn of Microsoft's calendar-2026 capex is simply higher component prices [[36]][r36].
- **Microsoft's positioning.** It presents itself as model-neutral, with models "swappable" behind its own platform layer [[61]][r61]. It also holds equity in both OpenAI and Anthropic [[4]][r4].

## 8. Earnings Quality

1. **OpenAI and Anthropic gains**
   - FY26 GAAP net income includes $4,963m ($0.67 per share) of net OpenAI gains [[3]][r3]. By quarter: a $3.1bn loss in Q1 [[17]][r17], a $7.6bn gain in Q2 [[18]][r18], and a gain in Q4. The Q2 gain mainly reflects dilution from the OpenAI recapitalisation [[9]][r9].
   - Non-GAAP figures exclude only OpenAI. They include the Q4 $3.2bn Anthropic gain [[4]][r4].
   - Discrete items added $0.27 to Q4 EPS [[4]][r4]. Removing them gives an underlying adjusted FY26 EPS of about $17.01 ($17.28 − $0.27), or 29.4x the share price.
2. **Capex paid late**
   - Property and equipment purchases still owed to suppliers rose from $6.9bn to $26.7bn [[1]][r1].
   - Capex on an accrued basis was about $135.7bn ($115.9bn + $19.8bn).
   - FCF adjusted for this payables build is about $47.2bn, not $67.0bn.
3. **Spending on components outside capex**
   - Cash used in "other investing primarily to facilitate the purchase of components" rose by $22.2bn [[1]][r1].
   - Related receivables were $17.8bn in March 2026, against $8.2bn in June 2025 [[9]][r9].
   - This outflow is not in the capex line. I could not find its absolute size, only the increase.
4. **Cash conversion is weakening.** Operating cash flow was 1.37x net income ($182.9bn / $133.7bn). But FCF was only 50% of GAAP net income, against 70% in FY25. Operating cash flow was also helped by "a decrease in cash used to pay income taxes" [[1]][r1].
5. **Stock-based compensation** was $12.4bn [[45]][r45], or 3.7% of revenue. Share issuance tied to it largely cancelled out buybacks (§11).
6. **Unearned revenue** (short-term) grew 13.0% ($72,965m vs $64,555m [[3]][r3]), slower than revenue. RPO growth of 84% is where the forward visibility sits.
7. **A third-party accruals analysis** flags a gap of about $33bn between earnings and cash flow that widened more than threefold in one year [[44]][r44].

**Overall:** earnings quality is good at the operating level, but GAAP net income is inflated by investment gains, and reported FCF is flattered by working-capital timing.

### Key question 3: How much of the growth is AI revenue that did not exist three years ago?

**What management discloses**
- In April 2026 Microsoft said its AI business had "surpassed an annual revenue run rate of $37 billion, up 123%" [[10]][r10].

**My calculations from that disclosure**
- Run-rate a year earlier: $37bn / 2.23 ≈ $16.6bn, so about $20.4bn of new annualised AI revenue.
- That compares with a FY26 revenue increase of $50.1bn ($331.8bn − $281.7bn), roughly **41% of the increase**. This mixes a point-in-time run-rate with a fiscal-year figure, so treat it as an estimate.
- AI is about **11.2% of revenue** ($37bn / $331.8bn).

**How new it is**
- Microsoft 365 Copilot launched in November 2023 [[56]][r56].
- Paid seats went from 15m (January 2026 [[55]][r55]) to 20m (Q3 [[56]][r56]) to more than 30m (Q4 [[4]][r4]).
- A third party estimates penetration at about 6.5% of the Microsoft 365 commercial base [[56]][r56].

**Frontier-lab exposure**
- About 10% of Microsoft Cloud revenue, roughly $21bn (inferred from "nearly 90%" coming from other customers [[5]][r5]), comes from frontier model companies. This revenue is new AI revenue, and some of it likely overlaps with the $37bn.

**What I could not source**
- Microsoft's definition of "AI business" and its AI revenue three years ago. As far as I could find, no FY23 figure is published, so I cannot make an exact three-year comparison.

**Conclusion:** roughly two-fifths of incremental growth, and a tenth of revenue, is AI revenue that effectively did not exist before 2023.

### Key question 2: What would have to be true for the current multiple to be justified?

At today's price the stock is on 27.9x trailing EPS, 23.7x EV/EBIT ($3,681bn / $155.2bn) and 19.0x EV/EBITDA. Holding everything else in the base case constant, I solved for the value of each input that makes the DCF equal $500.59:

| Input changed on its own | Needed | Base case |
|---|---|---|
| WACC | **8.75%** (equity risk premium about 3.6% at beta 1.0) | 9.55% |
| 10-year revenue growth | **13.1% a year** (FY36 revenue $1,135bn) | 11.2% ($963bn) |
| Long-run EBIT margin (FY31 onwards) | **52.7%** | 45.0% |
| Capex path | **55% of base** (about 45% lower) | — |
| Terminal return on new investment | **Cannot close the gap alone**: even an infinite return gives $469 | 25% |

In plain terms, the market is pricing one of three things:
- a compressed equity risk premium, even with the 10-year at 5.10%;
- AI capex that normalises much faster than guidance implies; or
- operating margins that move back towards Agents and Infra's 50.9% at group level.

A mix of smaller moves would also do it. For example, 8.95% WACC, 1.05x growth and a 46% margin get close to the price. I think this is possible but not the central case.
